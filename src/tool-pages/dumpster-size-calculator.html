How This Calculator Works

This calculator maps common Southwest Florida projects to the roll-off size that usually fits them, then flags the two things that change the answer: debris weight and site access. It never quotes a price, because the price depends on details only a hauler can confirm. Instead it points you to a sensible starting size so your call with a company is faster and more accurate.

Why the Result Is a Starting Point, Not a Final Answer

Two jobs that sound the same can need different boxes. A kitchen remodel with laminate counters is lighter than one with a tile floor and stone counters, and the tile is what decides the box. Heavy, dense debris, concrete, clay tile, brick, dirt, reaches a dumpster's weight limit long before it fills the space, so those loads go in a small box filled only partway, regardless of how much there is. The calculator points you toward the right size, and the hauler confirms it by weight. Read more in dumpster sizes explained.

Match the Box to the Weight

The single most expensive mistake in Southwest Florida is ordering by how big the pile looks. If your debris is dense, expect a smaller box than the volume suggests, and ask the hauler for the included weight allowance in tons and the per-ton overage rate. For light, bulky debris like furniture, drywall and shingles, volume is the limit and you size up. Compare all five sizes on the Dumpster Sizes hub.

Confirm Access Before You Book

On a tight canal-front lot or in an older, closely-spaced neighborhood, a 22-foot box may not physically fit, and the delivery truck needs a straight approach and clear overhead space. The calculator flags this for the larger project types, but it is worth confirming your site with the hauler for any size.
