# Dumpster Placement on a Narrow or Tight Lot

A narrow driveway, tight urban lot or close-set homes can make dumpster placement tricky. A smaller box and careful access planning usually solve it. This post covers tight-lot placement.

## Making It Fit a Tight Lot

On a narrow or tight lot, the constraints are the box's footprint and the truck's access. To make it work:

- **Choose a smaller box.** A 10- or 15-yard needs less length than a 20- or 30-yard, so it fits a short driveway, and all common sizes are about 8 feet wide.
- **Check the truck's approach.** The roll-off truck needs a straight, clear run to back in and overhead clearance; a tight or curved approach may rule out a large box.
- **Consider a swap** of a smaller box instead of one large one, if the job needs more volume than the small box holds.
- **Protect and position carefully** on a short driveway so you can still get in and out if needed.

If the lot genuinely cannot take a box, the street or swale with a permit is the fallback. Describe the lot and access to the hauler, who can recommend the size and placement that fit.
