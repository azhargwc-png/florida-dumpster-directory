# Can You Put Motor Oil in a Dumpster?

No. Used motor oil cannot go in a dumpster. It is a liquid (dumpsters take no liquids), it is restricted from landfill disposal by Florida law, and it is a recyclable product with dedicated drop-offs. Oil filters are also restricted. This post covers why used oil is excluded and where to recycle it in Southwest Florida.

## Why Used Oil Is Excluded

Three reasons combine. First, used oil is a liquid, and no liquids go in a roll-off, because they leak, add weight and contaminate the load. Second, used oil is specifically restricted from standard landfill disposal under Florida Statute 403.708, alongside lead-acid batteries and white goods. Third, used motor oil is highly recyclable, it can be re-refined into new oil or processed into fuel, so it belongs in the recycling stream, not a landfill. Oil filters retain oil and are restricted too.

## Where to Recycle Used Oil in Southwest Florida

Used oil has convenient drop-offs:

- **Auto-parts stores and quick-lube shops** commonly accept used motor oil (and often filters) for recycling, frequently free.
- **County household hazardous waste programs** take used oil: Lee County's Topaz Court facility, Charlotte County's Mini-Transfer facilities (where oil goes in a clear screw-top container), Collier County's drop-off centers, and Sarasota County's Chemical Collection Centers.
- **Cooking oil** is separate from motor oil but also has drop-offs; Charlotte County, for example, takes used cooking oil free at its Mini-Transfer facilities.

Store used oil in a clean, sealed container (ideally its original type) and keep it uncontaminated so it can be recycled.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I put used motor oil in a dumpster?**
No. Used motor oil is a liquid, and no liquids go in a roll-off, because they leak, add weight and contaminate the load. It is also specifically restricted from standard landfill disposal under Florida Statute 403.708, and it is highly recyclable, so it belongs in the recycling stream. Take used oil to an auto-parts store, a quick-lube shop, or your county hazardous-waste program instead. Oil filters retain oil and are restricted too, so they also stay out of the dumpster.

**Where do I recycle used oil in Southwest Florida?**
Several convenient options. Auto-parts stores and quick-lube shops commonly accept used motor oil and often filters for recycling, frequently free. County hazardous-waste programs also take it: Lee County's Topaz Court facility, Charlotte County's Mini-Transfer facilities (oil in a clear screw-top container), Collier County's drop-off centers, and Sarasota County's Chemical Collection Centers. Store used oil in a clean, sealed container and keep it uncontaminated so it can be re-refined or processed into fuel.

**Can I put oil filters in a dumpster?**
No. Used oil filters retain motor oil, so they carry the same restriction as the oil itself and cannot go in a dumpster or regular trash. Drain the filter first, then take it to a recycler that accepts filters; many auto-parts stores and quick-lube shops that take used oil also take filters. County hazardous-waste programs accept them as well. Keeping oil and filters out of the dumpster protects the load from contamination and keeps the recyclable oil in the proper stream.

**Is cooking oil the same as motor oil for disposal?**
No, they are separate streams, though neither goes in a dumpster. Used motor oil goes to auto-parts stores, quick-lube shops, or county hazardous-waste programs for re-refining. Used cooking oil has its own drop-offs; Charlotte County, for example, accepts used cooking oil free at its Mini-Transfer facilities, where it can be processed into products like biodiesel. Keep the two separate and uncontaminated, and never pour either into a dumpster, a drain, or onto the ground.
