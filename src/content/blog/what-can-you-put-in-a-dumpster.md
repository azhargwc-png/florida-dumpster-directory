# What Can You Put in a Dumpster? (And What You Can't)

Most of what a home project generates can go straight in a rented roll-off, but Florida law and hauler rules keep certain items out, and a prohibited item in the load can mean a surcharge or a refused pickup. This guide covers what you can put in a dumpster, what you cannot, and where the banned items actually have to go, with the Florida and Southwest Florida specifics that matter.

## The Quick Answer

A roll-off dumpster takes most construction, renovation, cleanout and general household debris. It does not take household hazardous waste, certain appliances, tires, or several other regulated items that Florida routes to separate programs. When in doubt, ask the hauler for their accepted-items list before you load, because they are the ones who pay the disposal facility and bear the contamination charge if something banned turns up.

## What You Can Put in a Dumpster

For a typical Southwest Florida home project, these go in without issue:

- **Construction and renovation debris:** drywall, lumber, framing, flooring, carpet, cabinetry, countertops, trim, and fixtures.
- **Roofing material:** asphalt shingles, and tile, though tile's weight means a smaller box (see the [roofing page](/roofing-dumpster-rental/)).
- **Household junk and cleanout debris:** furniture, boxes, non-electronic household goods, toys, clothing.
- **Concrete, brick, block and masonry:** accepted, but as heavy debris these go in a small box filled partway (see the [concrete page](/concrete-dumpster-rental/)).
- **Dirt, sand and sod:** accepted as heavy debris, sized by weight.
- **Yard and vegetative debris:** often accepted, but Florida treats it separately and it is usually kept in its own clean load (see the [yard waste page](/yard-waste-dumpster-rental/)).

The theme: ordinary, non-hazardous solid debris is fine. The weight, not the acceptability, is what decides the box for the heavy items.

## What You Cannot Put in a Dumpster

Florida regulates these items, and haulers keep them out of standard roll-offs:

- **Household hazardous waste:** paint, stains, solvents, motor oil, gasoline, pesticides, pool chemicals, and similar. These go to a county household hazardous waste program.
- **Batteries:** lead-acid (car) batteries and often other batteries are restricted from landfill disposal.
- **Tires:** almost universally excluded and handled through a separate recycling route.
- **Appliances with refrigerant:** refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners and similar, because the refrigerant must be recovered first. These "white goods" are restricted from standard landfill disposal.
- **Electronics (e-waste):** TVs, computers, monitors and similar often go to a separate e-waste program.
- **Propane tanks and other pressurized cylinders:** a fire hazard, excluded.
- **Medical waste, asbestos and other regulated materials:** handled under specific rules, never a roll-off.

Florida Statute 403.708 specifically restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil, yard trash and large appliances (white goods) from standard landfill disposal, which is the legal backbone of most of these exclusions.

## Where the Banned Items Actually Go

Keeping an item out of the dumpster does not mean you are stuck with it. Each Southwest Florida county runs collection programs:

- **Household hazardous waste** goes to the county HHW program: Lee County's Topaz Court facility, Charlotte County's Mini-Transfer HHW, Sarasota County's Chemical Collection Centers, and the Collier and DeSoto county equivalents.
- **Tires, batteries and electronics** are often accepted at these same county facilities or dedicated recycling events.
- **Appliances** may be collected by the county, taken by an appliance retailer on delivery of a replacement, or handled by a scrap metal recycler once the refrigerant is recovered.
- **Yard waste** goes through county curbside yard-waste programs or a mulching facility.

Your hauler can usually tell you the right local route for anything that cannot go in the box.

## Why It Matters: Contamination Charges and Refused Pickups

Haulers care about this because they pay the disposal facility, and a load contaminated with a prohibited item, say a few cans of paint or a refrigerator buried in the debris, can trigger a contamination surcharge, or the facility can refuse the load entirely. That cost comes back to you. A refused overweight or contaminated pickup also means the box sits longer, adding rental time. Keeping the load clean of prohibited items is both a legal and a financial issue.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I put a mattress in a dumpster?**
A mattress can usually go in a roll-off, but some haulers and facilities charge an extra fee for mattresses because they are bulky and awkward to process, and a few areas have mattress-recycling requirements. It is not a hazardous item, so it is not banned the way paint or batteries are, but ask the hauler whether a mattress carries a surcharge and whether they prefer it bagged, so there is no surprise on the bill.

**Can I put appliances in a dumpster in Florida?**
It depends on the appliance. Appliances without refrigerant, like a stove, dishwasher or washer, can often go in a roll-off, though they may be pulled for scrap-metal recycling. Appliances with refrigerant, refrigerators, freezers and air conditioners, are restricted, because the refrigerant must be professionally recovered first, and large appliances are limited from standard landfill disposal under Florida Statute 403.708. Ask the hauler how they handle appliances before loading one.

**Can I put paint in a dumpster?**
No. Paint is household hazardous waste and cannot go in a roll-off, whether it is liquid or not fully dried. Liquid paint, stains and solvents must go to a county household hazardous waste program, such as Lee County's Topaz Court facility, Charlotte County's Mini-Transfer HHW, or Sarasota County's Chemical Collection Centers. Completely dried-out latex paint is sometimes handled differently, but check your county's rule rather than assuming it can go in the box.

**What happens if I put a prohibited item in the dumpster?**
A prohibited item can trigger a contamination surcharge from the disposal facility, which the hauler passes on to you, or the facility can refuse the entire load, which means the box sits longer and adds rental time. Haulers pay the facility and bear the charge, which is why they ask you to keep banned items out. If you are unsure about an item, ask the hauler before you load it rather than risk the charge.
