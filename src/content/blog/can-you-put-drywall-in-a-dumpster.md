# Can You Put Drywall in a Dumpster?

Yes, drywall (sheetrock, gypsum board) is standard dumpster material and a routine part of any renovation or demolition load. The one thing people underestimate is its weight: drywall is denser than it looks, so a lot of it adds up fast. This post covers how to dumpster drywall, why it is heavy, and sizing a load.

## Yes, Drywall Goes In

Drywall is a common renovation and demolition debris, and it goes in a general roll-off load without issue. Torn-out walls and ceilings, offcuts from new hanging, and demo debris all go in the box. It is not hazardous and not restricted, so a bathroom, kitchen or whole-room renovation puts drywall straight in.

## It Is Heavier Than It Looks

Gypsum board is dense, so a stack of drywall sheets or a pile of demo debris weighs more than its volume suggests. A single sheet of half-inch drywall is already substantial, and a whole-house gut generates a lot of it. On its own, drywall does not need a separate box the way concrete does, but it does count toward the weight allowance, so a drywall-heavy demo can push a load toward its limit. Mention a full gut to the hauler so the weight allowance fits.

## Sizing a Drywall Load

For most renovations, drywall rides in the general debris box: a 15- or 20-yard for a room or two, a 20- or 30-yard for a larger renovation. The key is to account for the weight of the gypsum alongside the volume of everything else. A bathroom gut is light enough that drywall is a minor factor; a whole-house interior demolition is heavy enough that the hauler should set the allowance with the drywall tonnage in mind.

**Can drywall be recycled?**
Clean, unpainted new-construction drywall offcuts are sometimes recyclable, where a facility accepts them, since gypsum can be reprocessed. Demolition drywall that is painted, wet or mixed with other debris is usually landfilled. For most homeowner renovations, drywall simply rides in the general debris load and goes to disposal. If you have a large volume of clean offcuts and want to recycle, ask the hauler whether a gypsum-recycling route is available in your area.
