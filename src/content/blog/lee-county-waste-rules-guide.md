# Lee County Waste Rules: What You Can Dispose Of and What Needs a Dumpster

Lee County runs its own solid waste system, and knowing the rules tells you what regular collection handles, what has to go to a county facility, and when a project needs a roll-off dumpster. This guide covers the Topaz Court chemical and electronics facility, the county's mandatory construction-and-demolition recycling, hazardous-waste handling, and the point where a renovation or cleanout moves from curbside to a rented dumpster. It applies across Lee County, including Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Estero, Bonita Springs, Lehigh Acres, North Fort Myers, Sanibel and Fort Myers Beach.

## Regular Curbside Collection

Lee County residents get weekly curbside service for household garbage, recycling and yard waste. <cite index="59-1">The county uses single-stream recycling, which means all recyclable material can go in one container.</cite> Household garbage is handled as a standard can of limited weight, so it covers everyday trash, not renovation or cleanout debris.

For yard waste, the county asks residents to help its horticulture-waste program by setting material out properly. <cite index="59-1">Lee County uses yard waste to create mulch, and residents can help by using a trash can or paper bag when setting out yard waste.</cite> This matters for a dumpster decision: light routine yard trimmings fit curbside, while a large landscaping tear-out usually needs a roll-off.

## Household Chemical Waste and Electronics: The Topaz Court Facility

This is the single most important Lee County facility to know, because it is where everything that cannot go in a dumpster or the regular trash goes. <cite index="65-1">The Topaz Court Solid Waste Annex is at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers, FL 33966.</cite> <cite index="61-1">The Topaz Court Solid Waste Facility is available 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday and the first Saturday of each month (except holidays) from 8 a.m. to noon, with easy drive-through drop off.</cite>

The facility takes household chemical waste and electronics free of charge. <cite index="69-1">Lee County has a centrally located permanent collection facility at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers, and residents may dispose of qualifying items at no charge.</cite> Electronics have a dedicated route too. <cite index="59-1">Lee County residents can set old electronics at the curb with their trash, or bring them to the Household Chemical and Electronics Recycling Facility.</cite>

A useful detail for anyone doing a cleanout: the county runs a reuse program next door. <cite index="68-1">The Reuse Center, two doors down from the Topaz Court drop-off facility, offers latex paint and household chemicals back to residents at no charge, and inventory changes daily.</cite>

Two things the facility does not handle are worth noting. <cite index="74-1">The Lee County Solid Waste Department does not collect pharmaceuticals, and directs residents to call a local pharmacy or the Sheriff's office for a drop-off location.</cite> And it is a residential service. <cite index="73-1">Businesses must make special arrangements to dispose of their hazardous wastes, which is not accepted at the Topaz facility except during special collection events.</cite>

## Mandatory Construction and Demolition Recycling

Lee County is unusual in requiring construction debris to be recycled, which directly shapes how a renovation or build handles its debris. <cite index="71-1">Lee County has a mandatory construction and demolition debris requirement, backed by county ordinance.</cite> <cite index="71-1">Lee Ordinance 07-25 establishes mandatory recycling of commercial and multi-family residential solid waste and construction and demolition debris.</cite>

For a homeowner or contractor, the practical effect is that C and D debris from a project is expected to be handled through recycling-capable channels rather than simply landfilled, and a dumpster company serving Lee County will route the load accordingly. The county operates dedicated infrastructure for this. <cite index="67-1">Lee County's facilities include a Construction and Demolition Debris Recycling Facility and a Yard Waste Processing Operation.</cite>

## Hazardous Waste and Florida Landfill Bans

Beyond the county's own rules, Florida Statute 403.708 restricts certain items from standard landfill disposal, including lead-acid batteries, used oil, yard trash in some streams and large appliances (white goods). These never go in a roll-off dumpster. In Lee County they go to Topaz Court, with white goods handled separately from regular debris. During storm cleanup the county is explicit about this. <cite index="63-1">For storm debris, no white goods are accepted mixed in, and they must be placed curbside next to but separated from regular trash and other debris; no household chemical or hazardous waste is accepted, and it must be disposed of at the Topaz Court facility.</cite>

## When a Project Needs a Dumpster in Lee County

Regular collection covers routine household waste. A roll-off dumpster earns its place when a project produces more debris than curbside will take, or debris that curbside will not take at all:

- A kitchen, bath or whole-home renovation.
- A roof replacement.
- A garage, estate or whole-house cleanout.
- A construction or demolition project, where the C and D recycling requirement applies.
- A large landscaping tear-out beyond routine yard-waste collection.

For any of these, a dumpster rental company serving Lee County handles delivery, hauling and routing the load through the county's recycling system. Keep hazardous waste, electronics and white goods out of the box and take them to Topaz Court.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do I take household hazardous waste in Lee County?**
Household hazardous waste in Lee County goes to the Topaz Court Solid Waste Facility. It is at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers, FL 33966, open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday and the first Saturday of each month from 8 a.m. to noon, with free drive-through drop off for residents. It takes paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, motor oil, batteries and similar items, plus electronics. It does not take pharmaceuticals, which go to a pharmacy or the Sheriff's office, and it is a residential service, so businesses must arrange hazardous disposal separately.

**Does Lee County require construction debris to be recycled?**
Yes. Lee County has a mandatory construction and demolition debris recycling requirement, established by county ordinance, and operates a dedicated C and D recycling facility. For a renovation, build or demolition, this means the debris is expected to go through recycling-capable channels rather than straight to a landfill. A dumpster rental company serving Lee County routes the load accordingly, so renting a roll-off for your project keeps it compliant without you having to manage the recycling step yourself.

**Can I put electronics or appliances in a dumpster in Lee County?**
No. Electronics and large appliances (white goods) are kept out of roll-off dumpsters. In Lee County, electronics can be set at the curb with trash or taken to the Household Chemical and Electronics Recycling Facility at Topaz Court. Appliances with refrigerant, like refrigerators and air conditioners, need refrigerant recovery first, so ask the hauler. Florida Statute 403.708 also restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil and white goods from standard landfill disposal, so separate these before loading a dumpster.

**What size dumpster do I need for a Lee County project?**
It depends on the job and the debris weight. A cleanout or shingle re-roof usually fits a 20-yard box, a bathroom remodel a 15-yard, and a whole-home renovation a 20- or 30-yard. Heavy debris like concrete or tile goes in a smaller 10-yard box filled partway, since weight hits the limit before the box looks full. Confirm the size by weight with a dumpster company serving Lee County, and mention if the project is a construction or demolition job so the load is routed through the county's C and D recycling.
