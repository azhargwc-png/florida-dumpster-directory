# Port Charlotte Dumpster Permit and Placement Rules

Renting a dumpster in Port Charlotte, FL means working under Charlotte County rules, since Port Charlotte is unincorporated. The defining local factor is the swale: nearly every lot fronts one, and it is county right-of-way, not driveway. This guide covers permits, placement, the swale, and where your debris goes.

## Who Governs Placement in Port Charlotte

Port Charlotte is an unincorporated community, so Charlotte County governs placement and the county runs the solid-waste facilities. There is no separate city permit; the county's right-of-way rules apply. For a dumpster, the key question is whether the box sits on your private driveway or on the county right-of-way, which includes the swale.

## The Swale Is the Defining Factor

Port Charlotte is laid out in a dense grid of platted lots, and nearly every lot fronts a drainage swale, part of the county's extensive stormwater system. The swale is Charlotte County right-of-way, not an extension of your driveway, so:

- **On your own driveway:** a box generally needs no permit. This is the simplest and most common placement, and most Port Charlotte lots have driveway room.
- **On the swale or street:** a box needs a county right-of-way permit. A heavy box placed on a swale without one can damage the graded drainage channel and draw a fine.

Keep the box on your driveway unless you pull the county permit. Protect a paver driveway with boards under the roll-off's contact points.

## Where Your Debris Goes

Port Charlotte is home to the county's Mid-County Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility at 19765 Kenilworth Blvd, which takes recyclables, scrap metal, tires, oversized yard trimmings and household hazardous waste free for county residents with proof of residency. Keep hazardous waste, sharps, electronics and appliances out of the dumpster and take them there. For a renovation or cleanout, a dumpster hauler routes the general debris to the appropriate facility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Port Charlotte?**
It depends on where the box sits. Port Charlotte is unincorporated, so Charlotte County rules apply. A box entirely on your own driveway generally needs no permit, which is the most common placement. A box on the swale or street needs a county right-of-way permit, since the swale is county right-of-way rather than driveway. Keep the box on your driveway to avoid the permit, or pull a county right-of-way permit before placing it on the swale.

**Can I put a dumpster on the swale in Port Charlotte?**
Not without a county right-of-way permit. Nearly every Port Charlotte lot fronts a drainage swale, which is Charlotte County right-of-way, not an extension of your driveway, and part of the community's stormwater system. A heavy box there can damage the graded channel and draw a fine. Keep the box on your own driveway, where no permit is generally needed, or pull a county right-of-way permit. A local hauler experienced in Port Charlotte will know the current rule.

**Where does hazardous waste go in Port Charlotte?**
To the Mid-County Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility, conveniently located in Port Charlotte at 19765 Kenilworth Blvd. It takes household hazardous waste, sharps, recyclables, scrap metal, tires and oversized yard trimmings, free for county residents with proof of residency such as a driver's license or utility bill. None of these go in a dumpster, so set hazardous items aside before loading a roll-off for a Port Charlotte renovation or cleanout, and make one trip to the facility.

**What size dumpster do I need in Port Charlotte?**
It depends on the job and the debris weight. A cleanout or shingle re-roof usually fits a 20-yard box, a bathroom remodel a 15-yard, and a whole-home renovation a 20- or 30-yard. Heavy debris like concrete or tile goes in a smaller 10-yard box filled partway, since weight hits the limit before the box looks full. Confirm the size by weight with a dumpster company serving Port Charlotte, and keep the box on your driveway to avoid a swale permit.
