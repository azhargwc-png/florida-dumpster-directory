# Lee County Household Hazardous Waste Disposal

Household hazardous waste cannot go in a roll-off dumpster or the regular trash, and Lee County gives residents one free, permanent place to take it. This post covers what counts as household chemical waste, where it goes, the facility hours, and how to separate it before a dumpster job so your load stays clean and compliant.

## What Counts as Household Hazardous Waste

Household hazardous waste is any common household product that is flammable, toxic, corrosive or reactive. In practice that means paint and stains, solvents, gasoline and kerosene, motor oil and filters, pesticides and herbicides, pool chemicals, and batteries. These are exactly the items a dumpster hauler cannot accept, because they contaminate the load and are dangerous at the landfill.

Electronics are handled alongside hazardous waste as a separate recyclable stream, since televisions, computers and monitors contain materials that should be recovered rather than landfilled.

## Where It Goes: The Topaz Court Facility

Lee County operates a permanent, central drop-off for all of this. (cite index="69-1">Lee County has a centrally located permanent collection facility at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers, FL 33966, where residents may dispose of qualifying items at no charge.</cite> (cite index="61-1">The Topaz Court Solid Waste Facility is open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday and the first Saturday of each month (except holidays) from 8 a.m. to noon, with easy drive-through drop off.</cite>

The service covers the whole county, including the cities that contract with Lee County Solid Waste. (cite index="64-1">Cape Coral residents may dispose of household hazardous waste through Lee County's permanent Household Hazardous Waste Collection Facility at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers.</cite> (cite index="66-1">Estero's garbage, recycling and yard waste is handled by Lee County Solid Waste, and hazardous waste goes to the Lee County Household Chemical Waste Collection Facility at 6441 Topaz Court.</cite>

## A Reuse Program for Leftover Products

One detail worth knowing before you buy new paint or chemicals for a project: the county runs a free reuse program. (cite index="68-1">The Reuse Center, two doors down from the Topaz Court drop-off facility, offers latex paint and household chemicals back to residents at no charge, and inventory changes daily.</cite> It is a practical first stop for a renovation where you need primer, cleaner or similar.

## What the Facility Does Not Take

Two exclusions matter. (cite index="74-1">The Lee County Solid Waste Department does not collect pharmaceuticals, and directs residents to call a local pharmacy or the Sheriff's office for a drop-off location.</cite> And the facility is for residents, not businesses. (cite index="73-1">Businesses must make special arrangements to dispose of their hazardous wastes, which is not accepted at the Topaz facility except during special collection events.</cite>

## Keeping It Out of Your Dumpster

When you rent a roll-off for a renovation or cleanout, the hazardous items are the ones to pull out first, since a single banned item can trigger a contamination charge or a rejected load. Before loading:

- Set aside all paint, solvents, chemicals, oil and pool products for a Topaz Court trip.
- Separate electronics for the same facility or curbside e-waste pickup.
- Keep batteries and propane tanks out of the box entirely.
- Flag any refrigerant appliance to the hauler, since it needs refrigerant recovery first.

A single trip to Topaz Court before you start loading keeps the dumpster load clean and avoids extra charges.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Lee County hazardous waste facility address and hours?**
The Lee County household chemical waste facility is at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers, FL 33966. It is open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday and the first Saturday of each month (except holidays) from 8 a.m. to noon, with drive-through drop off. Disposal is free for Lee County residents, and the service covers cities that contract with Lee County Solid Waste, including Cape Coral and Estero. Businesses cannot use it except during special collection events and must arrange hazardous disposal separately.

**Can I throw away paint in Lee County?**
Not in a dumpster or regular trash. Wet or usable paint, stains and solvents go to the Topaz Court facility, free for residents. Lee County also runs a Reuse Center two doors down that offers latex paint and household chemicals back to residents at no charge, so it is worth checking there before buying new for a project. Fully dried latex paint is sometimes accepted in regular disposal, but confirm first. Never pour paint into a roll-off, since liquids leak, add weight and contaminate the load.

**Where do electronics go in Lee County?**
Electronics are handled alongside hazardous waste. Lee County residents can set old electronics at the curb with their trash, or bring them to the Household Chemical and Electronics Recycling Facility at Topaz Court, 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers. They are kept out of roll-off dumpsters because they contain materials that should be recovered rather than landfilled. Separate televisions, computers and monitors before loading a dumpster for a cleanout or renovation.

**Does household hazardous waste go in a dumpster?**
No. Paint, solvents, gasoline, motor oil, pesticides, pool chemicals, batteries and propane tanks cannot go in a roll-off dumpster, because they contaminate the load and are dangerous at the landfill. In Lee County they go to the Topaz Court facility free of charge. Putting a hazardous item in a dumpster can trigger a contamination charge or a rejected load, so set these items aside before you start loading and make one trip to Topaz Court.
