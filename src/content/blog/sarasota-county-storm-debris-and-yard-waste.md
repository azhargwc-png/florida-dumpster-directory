# Sarasota County Storm Debris and Yard Waste

Sarasota County handles storm debris and everyday yard waste under specific rules, including its distinctive Leaf It Out plastic-bag ban. Knowing them keeps your debris eligible for collection and tells you when a private dumpster is the right tool. This post covers storm debris, the yard-waste rules, the Leaf It Out campaign, and where a rented roll-off fits.

## Everyday Yard Waste: The Rules

Sarasota County collects residential yard waste weekly, but with firm limits. (cite index="120-1">Sarasota County collects residential yard waste weekly; it includes grass clippings, leaves, shrub trimmings, palm fronds, small tree limbs and branches, placed separately from garbage and recycling, and must be less than 4 feet in length, weigh less than 40 pounds, and be 16 inches or less in diameter.</cite> Anything beyond those limits, like a large tree removal or a landscaping tear-out, exceeds curbside and is a dumpster job.

## The Leaf It Out Plastic-Bag Rule

Sarasota County is distinctive for banning plastic bags from yard-waste collection. (cite index="111-1">Sarasota County's Leaf It Out campaign applies to yard waste; effective May 1, 2023, customers bringing yard waste in plastic bags to the landfill must empty the bags, and a minimum garbage fee applies to the plastic bags.</cite> (cite index="112-1">Yard waste placed curbside in plastic bags is no longer collected in unincorporated Sarasota County, and yard waste should not be raked or dumped into stormwater ditches or ponds.</cite> So use paper bags or reusable containers, and for a big job, a dumpster.

## Storm Debris

After a declared storm, Sarasota County coordinates collection of eligible storm debris from the right-of-way, separate from routine curbside service. As across the region, eligibility depends on separating debris into distinct piles, vegetative in one and construction and demolition in another, with hazardous waste and electronics kept out. Hazardous materials always go to a Chemical Collection Center. (cite index="124-1">Hazardous materials and electronics may be taken year-round to the Chemical Collection Center (North) at 8750 Bee Ridge Road, Sarasota, or (South) at 250 S. Jackson Road, Venice.</cite>

The county also runs periodic free community cleanup events where residents can drop household items and yard waste, including appliances and refrigerant units. (cite index="121-1">Sarasota County hosts free community cleanup events where residents can dispose of yard waste, tree trimmings, appliances, discarded household items, up to four small vehicle tires per household, and refrigerators containing refrigerant; hazardous waste and electronics go year-round to the Chemical Collection Centers or the Citizens' Convenience Center.</cite>

## When a Private Dumpster Is the Right Tool

The county's programs handle eligible yard waste, storm debris and cleanup-event drop-offs. A private dumpster is the right tool for:

- **A large landscaping tear-out** beyond the Leaf It Out curbside limits.
- **Private-property storm debris** you are clearing yourself.
- **Rebuild and repair debris** from fixing storm damage.
- **Debris you cannot wait on** or that exceeds event limits.

A private dumpster never competes with official collection. Use the county programs for eligible material, and a rented roll-off for the rest. Keep hazardous waste and electronics out, routing them to a Chemical Collection Center.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use plastic bags for yard waste in Sarasota County?**
No. Under Sarasota County's Leaf It Out rule, effective May 1, 2023, yard waste in plastic bags is no longer collected curbside in unincorporated Sarasota County, and bags brought to the landfill must be emptied, with a minimum garbage fee for the plastic. Use paper bags or reusable containers instead, keeping each piece less than 4 feet long, under 40 pounds, and 16 inches or less in diameter. For a large tree removal or landscaping tear-out beyond those limits, a rented dumpster is the practical route.

**How does Sarasota County handle storm debris?**
After a declared storm, Sarasota County coordinates collection of eligible storm debris from the right-of-way, separate from routine curbside service. Eligibility depends on separating debris into distinct piles, vegetative in one and construction and demolition in another, with hazardous waste and electronics kept out and routed to a Chemical Collection Center. The county also runs periodic free community cleanup events for yard waste, appliances and household items. The storm program runs for a limited window, so check current notices after an event.

**Should I rent a dumpster or use Sarasota County collection?**
Use both for what each is meant for. The county collects weekly yard waste within size limits, coordinates storm-debris collection after a declared event, and runs free community cleanup events. A private dumpster is for a large landscaping tear-out beyond the Leaf It Out limits, private-property or rebuild debris, and anything you cannot wait on. A private dumpster never competes with official collection, so many homeowners use the county programs for routine and eligible material and a roll-off for big jobs and rebuild work.

**What can I bring to a Sarasota County community cleanup event?**
At the county's free community cleanup events, residents can dispose of yard waste and tree trimmings, appliances, discarded household items, up to four small vehicle tires per household, and refrigerators containing refrigerant. Hazardous waste and electronics are not part of the events and go year-round to a Chemical Collection Center or the Citizens' Convenience Center instead. For debris beyond what an event accepts, or outside event timing, a rented dumpster handles the job on your own schedule.
