# Collier County Household Hazardous Waste Disposal

Household hazardous waste cannot go in a roll-off dumpster or the regular trash, and Collier County accepts it free at its recycling drop-off centers. This post covers what counts, where it goes, which items the centers take beyond paint, and how to separate everything before a dumpster job so your load stays clean.

## What Counts as Household Hazardous Waste

Household hazardous products are the common home items that are flammable, toxic, corrosive or reactive. In Collier County that means paints and oils, fluorescent bulbs, herbicides and pesticides, pool chemicals, automotive products and electronics. (cite index="101-1">Collier County residents can drop off household chemicals such as oils, paints, fluorescent bulbs, electronics, herbicides and pesticides.</cite> These are exactly the items a dumpster hauler cannot accept.

## Where It Goes: The Drop-Off Centers

Collier County makes this easy by combining hazardous-waste collection with its recycling centers. (cite index="99-1">Collier County operates several recycling drop-off centers to collect and properly dispose of household hazardous waste, and recyclable items may also be dropped off there.</cite> Disposal is free. (cite index="94-1">Residents may bring their household hazardous products to any county-operated recycling drop-off center for free disposal.</cite>

Center locations include Naples, Marco Island and the Northeast (Tim Nance) center. (cite index="110-1">Drop-off centers include the Tim Nance Center at 9950 Goodlette-Frank Rd, Naples; a Naples center at 2640 Corporate Flight Dr; and Marco Island at 990 Chalmer Dr.</cite> There is also a dedicated hazardous materials center at the landfill. (cite index="105-1">The Collier County Landfill and Hazardous Materials Collection Center is at 3730 White Lake Blvd, open Monday through Saturday.</cite>

## Keeping It Out of Your Dumpster

When you rent a roll-off for a renovation or cleanout, the hazardous items are the first to pull out, since one banned item can trigger a contamination charge. Before loading:

- Set aside paint, oils, automotive products, herbicides, pesticides and pool chemicals for a drop-off center.
- Separate electronics and fluorescent bulbs for the same centers.
- Keep batteries and propane tanks out of the box.
- Flag any refrigerant appliance to the hauler, since it needs refrigerant recovery first.

One trip to a Collier County drop-off center before you load keeps the dumpster load clean and compliant with Florida Statute 403.708, which restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil and large appliances from standard landfill disposal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do I take household hazardous waste in Collier County?**
To any Collier County recycling drop-off center, free of charge. Locations include the Tim Nance (Northeast) center at 9950 Goodlette-Frank Rd, the Naples center at 2640 Corporate Flight Dr, and Marco Island at 990 Chalmer Dr, plus the Hazardous Materials Collection Center at the landfill, 3730 White Lake Blvd. They take oils, paints, fluorescent bulbs, electronics, herbicides, pesticides, pool chemicals and automotive products. None of these go in a dumpster.

**Is hazardous waste disposal free in Collier County?**
Yes. Collier County residents may bring their household hazardous products to any county-operated recycling drop-off center for free disposal. This covers the common items a home generates: paints, oils, chemicals, bulbs, batteries and electronics. The convenience of combining recycling and hazardous-waste drop-off at the same centers means one trip handles both. Businesses follow different rules and may need to use the hazardous materials collection center at the landfill under small-quantity-generator terms.

**Can I put electronics in a dumpster in Collier County?**
No. Electronics are kept out of roll-off dumpsters and are accepted at Collier County's recycling drop-off centers alongside household hazardous waste. Televisions, computers, monitors and similar devices contain materials that should be recovered rather than landfilled. Separate them before loading a dumpster for a cleanout or renovation, and drop them at a Naples, Marco Island or Northeast center. Appliances with refrigerant, like refrigerators, need refrigerant recovery first, so ask the hauler.

**Does household hazardous waste go in a dumpster?**
No. Paints, oils, automotive products, herbicides, pesticides, pool chemicals, fluorescent bulbs, batteries and propane tanks cannot go in a roll-off dumpster, because they contaminate the load and are dangerous at the landfill. In Collier County they go free to a recycling drop-off center. Putting a hazardous item in a dumpster can trigger a contamination charge or a rejected load, so set these aside before loading and make one trip to a drop-off center.
