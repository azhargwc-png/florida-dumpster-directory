# Can You Put Propane Tanks in a Dumpster?

No. Propane tanks, from the small grill cylinders to larger ones, must never go in a rental dumpster. A pressurized tank, even one that seems empty, can explode when compacted in a truck or exposed to heat, which is a serious danger to crews. This post covers why propane is excluded and where to take or exchange a tank in Southwest Florida.

## Why Propane Tanks Are a Hard No

A propane cylinder holds flammable gas under pressure. In a dumpster, it faces exactly the conditions that cause a tank to rupture or explode: compaction in the garbage truck, puncture by other debris, and Florida's heat. Even a tank you believe is empty usually holds residual gas and pressure. Waste crews are seriously endangered by propane tanks in loads, so they are a firm exclusion, in the same category as other pressurized and hazardous items.

## Where Propane Tanks Go

You have a few good routes:

- **Exchange programs.** The most common route for standard 20 lb grill cylinders is a tank-exchange cage at a hardware store, gas station or grocery; you can turn in an old tank when swapping for a full one.
- **Propane dealers and refill stations.** These often take back old or expired cylinders.
- **County household hazardous waste programs.** Lee, Charlotte, Collier and Sarasota county facilities accept propane cylinders among their hazardous items; Sarasota County's collection-event lists include propane cylinders.
- **Some retailers** that sell propane will take old tanks for recycling.

Never put a propane tank in a dumpster, the regular trash, or a recycling bin.

## Larger Tanks

Bigger tanks (100 lb cylinders or the large stationary tanks for home heating or whole-house propane) are handled by your propane supplier, who will pick up, purge and remove them properly. Do not attempt to dispose of a large tank yourself, and never put one near a dumpster.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I throw a propane tank in a dumpster?**
No, never. A propane cylinder holds flammable gas under pressure, and in a dumpster it faces the exact conditions that cause a rupture or explosion: compaction in the garbage truck, puncture by other debris, and Florida's heat. Even a tank you think is empty usually holds residual gas and pressure. Propane tanks seriously endanger waste crews, so they are a firm exclusion from any dumpster, trash or recycling bin. Use an exchange program, a propane dealer, or your county hazardous-waste program.

**Where do I dispose of a propane tank in Southwest Florida?**
For a standard 20 lb grill cylinder, the easiest route is a tank-exchange cage at a hardware store, gas station or grocery, where you turn in the old tank when swapping for a full one. Propane dealers and refill stations often take back old or expired cylinders, and the Lee, Charlotte, Collier and Sarasota county hazardous-waste programs accept propane cylinders. Never put a tank in a dumpster, regular trash or recycling bin. Larger tanks are handled by your propane supplier.

**Is an empty propane tank safe to throw away?**
No. Even a tank you believe is empty usually holds residual gas and pressure, which is enough to be dangerous when the tank is compacted or punctured in a garbage truck or exposed to heat. There is no safe way to put a propane cylinder in a dumpster or trash. Treat an "empty" tank the same as a full one: exchange it, return it to a propane dealer, or take it to a county hazardous-waste program. Exchange cages make this easy for standard grill cylinders.

**How do I dispose of a large propane tank?**
Larger cylinders (100 lb) and the big stationary tanks used for home heating or whole-house propane are handled by your propane supplier. They will pick up, purge and remove the tank properly, since it requires safe depressurizing and handling. Do not attempt to dispose of a large tank yourself, cut it, or put it near a dumpster. Contact the supplier that services or filled the tank, and they will arrange proper removal.
