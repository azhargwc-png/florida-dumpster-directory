# Charlotte County Household Hazardous Waste Disposal

Household hazardous waste cannot go in a roll-off dumpster or the regular trash, and Charlotte County collects it year-round at its two Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities. This post covers what counts, where it goes, the proof-of-residency rule, the items beyond paint and chemicals that these facilities take, and how to separate everything before a dumpster job.

## What Counts as Household Hazardous Waste

A product is household hazardous waste if it is corrosive, toxic, flammable or reactive. (cite index="84-1">A product is considered hazardous if it is corrosive, toxic, flammable, or reacts or explodes.</cite> In a typical home that means paint and stains, antifreeze, cleaners, gasoline and kerosene, pesticides and herbicides, pool chemicals, motor oil and filters, and batteries. The Punta Gorda sanitation guidance lists these plainly. (cite index="91-1">Items like antifreeze, batteries, cleaners, flares, gasoline, kerosene, pesticides, herbicides, pool chemicals, mercury, motor oil, used oil filters, paint, stains and varnishes are considered Household Hazardous Waste and are not collected curbside; homeowners must transport them to a Charlotte County Mini-Transfer Station.</cite>

## Where It Goes

Charlotte County collects hazardous waste at both Mini-Transfer facilities. (cite index="84-1">The Charlotte County Solid Waste Division offers year-round household hazardous waste collection at its recycling facilities in Port Charlotte and Englewood, and proof of residency is required.</cite> (cite index="87-1">The Mid-County facility is at 19765 Kenilworth Blvd., Port Charlotte, and the West Charlotte facility is at 7070 Environmental Way, Englewood.</cite>

It is a residential service. (cite index="76-1">Charlotte County only offers these services for residential households; businesses must hire a hazardous hauler.</cite> And proof of residency is required. (cite index="91-1">The facility is free to use for Charlotte County residents, with proof of residency required such as a valid driver's license, utility bill or tax bill, and Post Office Box addresses are not accepted.</cite>

## Beyond Paint: Sharps, Mercury, Bulbs and Cooking Oil

Charlotte County's facilities take several streams that surprise people:

- (cite index="75-1">Households can take used hypodermic needles (sharps) to the Mini-Transfer Facilities</cite> alongside their hazardous waste.
- (cite index="77-1">Households can recycle mercury devices and fluorescent light bulbs at the Mini-Transfer Facilities.</cite>
- (cite index="76-1">Residents can dispose of used cooking oil free of charge at both Mid-County and West Charlotte Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities.</cite>
- Single-use household batteries can go in the trash, but rechargeable and lead-acid batteries go to the facility. (cite index="76-1">Take batteries to a retailer or to a Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility; regular single-use household batteries can be thrown in your trash.</cite>

## Keeping It Out of Your Dumpster

When you rent a roll-off for a renovation or cleanout, pull the hazardous items first, since one banned item can trigger a contamination charge. Before loading:

- Set aside paint, chemicals, oil, antifreeze and pool products for a Mini-Transfer trip.
- Separate sharps, mercury devices and fluorescent bulbs for the same facilities.
- Keep rechargeable and lead-acid batteries and propane tanks out of the box.
- Flag any refrigerant appliance to the hauler.

One trip to a Mini-Transfer facility before loading keeps the dumpster load clean.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do I take household hazardous waste in Charlotte County?**
To the Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities: Mid-County at 19765 Kenilworth Blvd., Port Charlotte, or West Charlotte at 7070 Environmental Way, Englewood. Both offer year-round household hazardous waste collection for residents, free with proof of residency such as a driver's license, utility bill or tax bill (no Post Office Box addresses). They take paint, chemicals, antifreeze, pesticides, pool chemicals, oil, batteries, sharps, mercury devices, fluorescent bulbs and cooking oil. The service is residential only, so businesses must hire a hazardous waste hauler.

**Can I take sharps or needles to a Charlotte County facility?**
Yes. Households can take used hypodermic needles (sharps) to the Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities in Port Charlotte and Englewood, alongside their household hazardous waste. This keeps needles out of both the regular trash and any dumpster, where they are a hazard to collection crews. Container them safely for transport. The facilities also take mercury devices, fluorescent bulbs and used cooking oil, all free for residents with proof of residency.

**Can I throw away cooking oil or paint in Charlotte County?**
Not in a dumpster or regular trash. Used cooking oil can be dropped free at both the Mid-County and West Charlotte Mini-Transfer facilities. Paint, stains and varnishes are household hazardous waste and go to the same facilities, which also run a reuse program for usable products. Never pour oil or paint into a roll-off, since liquids leak, add weight and contaminate the load. Separate them for a Mini-Transfer trip before you start a dumpster job.

**Does household hazardous waste go in a dumpster in Charlotte County?**
No. Paint, chemicals, antifreeze, pesticides, pool chemicals, oil, batteries, sharps, mercury devices and fluorescent bulbs all stay out of a roll-off dumpster and go to a Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility instead. Putting any of them in a dumpster can trigger a contamination charge or a rejected load. Separate these items before loading and make one trip to the Port Charlotte or Englewood facility, free for residents with proof of residency.
