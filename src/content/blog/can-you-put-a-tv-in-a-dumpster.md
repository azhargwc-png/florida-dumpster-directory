# Can You Put a TV in a Dumpster?

A television should not go in a rental dumpster. TVs are e-waste, and older sets in particular contain hazardous materials, so they go through electronics recycling instead. This post covers the TV rule, why old CRT sets are a special concern, and where to recycle a television in Southwest Florida.

## TVs Are E-Waste

A television is an electronic device, so it follows the same rule as computers and monitors: it is handled through e-waste recycling, not a general dumpster load. TVs contain recoverable materials and, depending on the type, hazardous components, so haulers and county programs keep them out of the box. Whether it is a cleanout, a move or an upgrade, set the old TV aside for e-waste rather than the dumpster.

## Old CRT TVs Are a Special Concern

The bulky older tube-style televisions (CRTs) deserve a note. A CRT contains leaded glass, a significant amount of lead, which makes it clearly hazardous and absolutely unsuitable for a dumpster or regular trash. Flat-screen TVs (LCD, LED, plasma) are lighter and use different materials, but they are still e-waste and still go through recycling. Both types stay out of the box; the CRT is simply the more hazardous of the two.

## Where to Recycle a TV in Southwest Florida

Televisions go through the same county e-waste routes as other electronics:

- **Lee County:** the Household Chemical and Electronics Recycling Facility at Topaz Court, or curbside with trash per county guidance.
- **Charlotte County:** the Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities.
- **Collier County:** the recycling drop-off centers.
- **Sarasota County:** the Chemical Collection Centers, year-round.

A TV is bulky, so handling it through e-waste also saves the dumpster space it would otherwise eat up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I throw a TV in a dumpster?**
No. A television is an electronic device, so it is handled through e-waste recycling rather than a general dumpster load. TVs contain recoverable materials and, depending on the type, hazardous components, so haulers and county programs keep them out of the box. Whether you are doing a cleanout, a move or an upgrade, set the old TV aside and take it to your county's e-waste route rather than putting it in the dumpster.

**Are old CRT TVs different from flat-screens?**
For disposal, yes. An old tube-style CRT television contains leaded glass, a significant amount of lead, making it clearly hazardous and absolutely unsuitable for a dumpster or regular trash. Flat-screen TVs (LCD, LED, plasma) are lighter and use different materials, but they are still e-waste and still go through recycling. Both types stay out of the dumpster; the CRT is simply the more hazardous. Both go to your county's electronics-recycling route.

**Where do I recycle a TV in Southwest Florida?**
Through the same county e-waste routes as other electronics. Lee County takes TVs at the Household Chemical and Electronics Recycling Facility at Topaz Court, or curbside with trash per county guidance. Charlotte County takes them at its Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities, Collier County at its recycling drop-off centers, and Sarasota County at the Chemical Collection Centers year-round. A TV is bulky, so recycling it also saves the dumpster space it would otherwise take up.

**Why can't a television go in the trash or dumpster?**
Because a TV is e-waste, containing materials worth recovering and, especially in older CRT sets, hazardous components like leaded glass that should not go to a landfill. Routing TVs through electronics recycling recovers the materials and keeps the hazardous parts out of the landfill. It is the same reason computers and monitors are excluded. Set the TV aside for your county's e-waste program rather than the dumpster or curbside trash (except where county guidance specifically allows curbside e-waste).
