# Can You Put Concrete in a Dumpster?

Yes, concrete goes in a dumpster, but it is the material most likely to catch people out on weight. Concrete is so dense that a box filled with it can blow past the weight limit while looking nearly empty, so haulers handle it with a small box filled only partway, and often a dedicated clean-concrete load. This post covers how to dumpster concrete, clean vs mixed loads, and why size and weight matter here more than anywhere else.

## Yes, But Weight Is Everything

Concrete, along with brick, block, stone and asphalt, is among the densest debris you can generate. A dumpster has both a volume limit (how much fits) and a weight limit (how many tons the truck can carry), and with concrete the weight limit is reached first, by a wide margin. A 10-yard box of concrete can hit the weight ceiling when it is only a fraction full. So the rule is: use a small box, and fill it only partway. See the [concrete dumpster page](/concrete-dumpster-rental/) for how this is handled.

## Clean Loads vs Mixed Loads

Haulers strongly prefer a clean concrete load, meaning concrete (and similar masonry) with no wood, trash, rebar-tangled debris or other contamination. Two reasons: clean concrete can be crushed and recycled into aggregate, and a clean heavy load is easier to price and handle than a mixed one. If you throw a little concrete into a general renovation load, you risk pushing that whole load overweight and losing the recycling benefit. Keep concrete in its own box when you have more than a trace.

## How Much Fits

Because weight governs, think in tons, not volume. A modest slab, a section of driveway, or a patio tear-out produces a lot of weight in a small footprint. Describe the job to the hauler, including the square footage and thickness if you know them, so they bring the right small box and set a realistic weight allowance. Overfilling a concrete box past the allowance leads to an overage charge or a load the truck cannot safely lift.

## In Southwest Florida Specifically

Concrete and masonry are everywhere in Southwest Florida construction, from block walls to paver and slab driveways to pool decks. A driveway or pool-deck tear-out is a classic concrete-dumpster job. Keep it a clean load, use a 10-yard box filled partway, and confirm the weight allowance, since these projects reach the limit fast.

**Why does concrete need a small dumpster?**
Because a dumpster has a weight limit as well as a volume limit, and concrete reaches the weight limit long before the box looks full. This keeps the load within the truck's safe lifting capacity and within your weight allowance.
