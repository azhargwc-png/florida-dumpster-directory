# Dumpster for a Demolition Project

A demolition generates a large volume of mixed, often heavy debris at once. Sizing and separating it is the key. This post covers demolition dumpster use.

## Handling Demolition Debris

Demolition, of a structure, an addition, a pool or an interior, produces a burst of mixed debris: wood, drywall, roofing, flooring, and often concrete, block and masonry. To handle it:

- **Size for the burst.** Demolition fills a box fast, so a 20- or 30-yard for the general debris, or scheduled swaps, suits most jobs; a large structure may need the 40-yard or multiple hauls.
- **Separate the heavy material.** Concrete, block and masonry go in a dedicated small box filled partway, apart from the light demolition debris.
- **Check for asbestos first** in a pre-1980s structure, which must be tested and abated before demolition (see the asbestos post).
- **Mind the weight** on mixed demolition debris, which can be heavier than it looks.

See the [demolition dumpster page](/demolition-dumpster-rental/). Describe the demolition to the hauler so they size the boxes and plan swaps.
