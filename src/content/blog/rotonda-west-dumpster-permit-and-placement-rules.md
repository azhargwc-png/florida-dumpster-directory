# Rotonda West Dumpster Permit and Placement Rules

Rotonda West, FL is a deed-restricted planned community of circular canals in Charlotte County, and its dumpster rules stack two layers: the Charlotte County swale rule and the Rotonda West Association's own restrictions. This guide covers both, plus where your debris goes.

## Two Layers of Rules

Rotonda West is unincorporated, so Charlotte County governs placement, but it is also deed-restricted, so the Rotonda West Association has its own rules. Both apply:

**Layer 1, the county swale rule.** Rotonda West's distinctive canal-and-swale design means nearly every lot fronts a drainage swale, which is Charlotte County right-of-way, not driveway. A box on the swale or street needs a county right-of-way permit; a box on your own driveway generally needs none.

**Layer 2, the deed restrictions.** The Rotonda West Association may set its own rules on dumpster placement, duration and screening. Check the association's rules before booking, in addition to the county rule.

So the safest placement is on your own driveway, which clears the county permit question, while you still confirm the association's rules on how long the box can stay and whether it needs screening.

## Driveway Protection

Rotonda West lots can have paver or finished driveways. Protect them with boards under the roll-off's contact points to prevent cracking or staining, and confirm the delivery truck's access on the community's curved streets.

## Where Your Debris Goes

Rotonda West uses the Charlotte County facilities. The West Charlotte Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility at 7070 Environmental Way, Englewood is the nearest, taking household hazardous waste, sharps, recyclables, scrap metal and tires free for county residents with proof of residency. Keep hazardous waste and appliances out of the dumpster and take them there. A dumpster hauler handles the general debris.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Rotonda West?**
Two things to check. Rotonda West is unincorporated, so Charlotte County rules apply: a box on your own driveway generally needs no permit, while a box on the swale or street needs a county right-of-way permit. It is also deed-restricted, so the Rotonda West Association may have its own rules on placement, duration and screening. Check both the county rule and the association before booking. Keeping the box on your driveway clears the county permit question.

**Can I put a dumpster on the swale in Rotonda West?**
Not without a county right-of-way permit. Rotonda West's canal-and-swale design means nearly every lot fronts a drainage swale, which is Charlotte County right-of-way, not an extension of your driveway. A heavy box there can damage the graded channel and draw a fine. Keep the box on your own driveway, where no permit is generally needed, or pull a county right-of-way permit. Also check the Rotonda West Association's rules, since the deed restrictions apply on top of the county rule.

**Does the Rotonda West Association have dumpster rules?**
It may. As a deed-restricted planned community, Rotonda West can have its own association rules on where a dumpster can sit, how long it can stay, and whether it needs screening, on top of the Charlotte County swale and right-of-way rules. Check the association's current rules before booking, confirm placement, and clear any duration limit with both the association and the hauler. The county permit rules for the swale still apply in addition to the association's.

**Where does hazardous waste go in Rotonda West?**
To the Charlotte County Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities. The nearest is West Charlotte at 7070 Environmental Way, Englewood, which takes household hazardous waste, sharps, recyclables, scrap metal and tires, free for county residents with proof of residency. The Mid-County facility in Port Charlotte is the other option. None of these items go in a dumpster, so set them aside before loading a roll-off for a Rotonda West renovation or cleanout.
