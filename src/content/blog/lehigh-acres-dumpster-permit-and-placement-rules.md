# Lehigh Acres Dumpster Permit and Placement Rules

Renting a dumpster in Lehigh Acres, FL means knowing who governs placement, where a box can sit, and where your debris goes. This guide covers the permit question, placement, and the local Lee County facilities.

## Who Governs Placement in Lehigh Acres

Lehigh Acres is an unincorporated community in Lee County, so Lee County governs placement. A box on a public right-of-way follows county rules; a box on your own driveway generally needs no permit.

## Placement and Local Factors

Lehigh Acres is a large, spread-out residential community of platted lots, many with room for private driveway placement. It has grown with new and renovated housing, a steady source of dumpster demand.

**On your own driveway:** a box entirely on your private driveway usually needs no permit, which is the simplest option. Protect a paver or finished driveway with boards under the roll-off's contact points.

**On the street or right-of-way:** a box there follows the rules of whoever owns the road, so confirm the current requirement before placing one.

## Where Your Debris Goes

Lehigh Acres uses the Lee County facilities. Household hazardous waste and electronics go free to the Topaz Court facility at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers, and the county's mandatory construction-and-demolition recycling applies to renovation debris. Keep hazardous waste, electronics and appliances out of the dumpster.

Yes. Lee County's mandatory construction-and-demolition debris recycling requirement applies countywide, including Lehigh Acres. For a renovation, build or demolition, the debris is expected to go through recycling-capable channels rather than straight to a landfill. It depends on the job and the debris weight. A cleanout or shingle re-roof usually fits a 20-yard box, a bathroom remodel a 15-yard, and a whole-home renovation a 20- or 30-yard. Heavy debris like concrete or tile goes in a smaller 10-yard box filled partway, since weight hits the limit before the box looks full.
