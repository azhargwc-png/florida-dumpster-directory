# How to Dispose of Hazardous Waste in Southwest Florida

Household hazardous waste, paint, chemicals, oil, batteries, pool chemicals, propane, never goes in a rental dumpster, so this is the guide to where it actually goes in each Southwest Florida county. Knowing your county's free program means you can clear the hazardous items before a dumpster job and keep your load clean. This post pulls the county facilities together in one place.

## What Counts as Household Hazardous Waste

Household hazardous waste is any common home product that is flammable, toxic, corrosive or reactive: paint and stains, solvents, gasoline and kerosene, motor oil and filters, pesticides and herbicides, pool chemicals, automotive fluids, aerosols, and batteries. Electronics are handled alongside it as a separate recyclable stream. All of it stays out of the dumpster.

## By County

**Lee County.** The Topaz Court facility at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers, takes household chemical waste and electronics free for residents, and runs a Reuse Center offering usable products back. It covers cities that contract with Lee County Solid Waste, like Cape Coral and Estero. Pharmaceuticals go to a pharmacy or the Sheriff's office instead.

**Charlotte County.** The Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities, Mid-County at 19765 Kenilworth Blvd, Port Charlotte, and West Charlotte at 7070 Environmental Way, Englewood, take hazardous waste, sharps, mercury devices, fluorescent bulbs and used cooking oil, free with proof of residency.

**Collier County.** Any county recycling drop-off center takes household hazardous products free, including the Tim Nance (Northeast), Naples and Marco Island centers, plus the Hazardous Materials Collection Center at the landfill, 3730 White Lake Blvd.

**Sarasota County.** The Chemical Collection Centers, North at 8750 Bee Ridge Road, Sarasota, and South at 250 S. Jackson Road, Venice, take hazardous waste and electronics free for all residents year-round, supplying The Re-Uz-It Shop. Pharmaceuticals are no longer accepted there.

**DeSoto and Hendry counties.** As rural counties, these use periodic collection events or specific arrangements rather than large permanent facilities; check with the county for current options.

## The Workflow Before a Dumpster Job

1. **Sort the hazardous items out first,** into a box or bin kept separate from the debris.
2. **Make one trip** to your county facility with everything.
3. **Then load the dumpster** with the clean, non-hazardous debris.

This keeps a banned item from triggering a contamination charge and keeps you from having to deal with it after the fact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do I dispose of hazardous waste in Southwest Florida?**
At your county's free household hazardous waste program. Lee County uses the Topaz Court facility at 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers; Charlotte County the Mini-Transfer facilities in Port Charlotte and Englewood; Collier County any recycling drop-off center or the Hazardous Materials Collection Center at the landfill; and Sarasota County the Chemical Collection Centers in Sarasota and Venice. DeSoto and Hendry, being rural, use periodic events. All take paint, chemicals, oil, batteries and similar items free for residents, and none of it goes in a dumpster.

**Is hazardous waste disposal free in Southwest Florida?**
Yes, for residents, at the county programs. Lee, Charlotte, Collier and Sarasota counties all accept household hazardous waste free from residents (Charlotte and some others require proof of residency). The programs exist precisely so residents have a safe, no-cost alternative to putting these items in the trash or a dumpster. Businesses follow different rules and generally must arrange hazardous disposal through a licensed hauler rather than the residential programs.

**What should I take to a hazardous waste facility?**
Paint and stains, solvents, gasoline and kerosene, motor oil and filters, pesticides and herbicides, pool chemicals, automotive fluids, aerosols, and batteries (rechargeable, lithium and vehicle). Electronics go to the same facilities in most counties. Propane cylinders are accepted at the county programs too. Essentially, anything flammable, toxic, corrosive or reactive, plus e-waste, goes to the county facility rather than the dumpster. Pharmaceuticals and sharps have their own routes, which vary by county.

**Why can't hazardous waste go in a dumpster?**
Because it contaminates the load, endangers crews, and in many cases is legally restricted from landfill disposal (used oil and lead-acid batteries under Florida Statute 403.708, for example). A single hazardous item in a dumpster can trigger a contamination charge or cause the whole load to be rejected at the landfill. The county programs give you a free, safe alternative, so sort hazardous items out before a dumpster job and make one trip to your county facility with them.
