# Hendry County Waste and Curbside Debris Rules

Hendry County, centered on LaBelle, is a rural agricultural county, and its waste rules include a specific curbside construction-debris limit that pushes most projects toward a dumpster. Knowing the rules tells you what curbside covers, where debris and hazardous waste go, and when an LaBelle-area project needs a roll-off. This guide covers the curbside limit, hazardous-waste handling, and the point where a project moves to a rented dumpster.

## The Curbside Construction-Debris Limit

This is the key Hendry County rule. In Hendry County, residents may set out only a limited amount of construction debris at the curb, with larger amounts going to a transfer station or a rented dumpster. Because the curbside allowance is small, anything beyond a very minor repair usually needs a dumpster. The practical effect is that a renovation, re-roof or any real construction project in LaBelle or unincorporated Hendry County is a dumpster job from the start, rather than something curbside can absorb over several weeks. A local hauler will know the current limit and the transfer-station options.

## Curbside and Rural Collection

Hendry County handles routine residential garbage and recycling through its collection arrangements, with the City of LaBelle governing inside the city and the county governing the unincorporated areas, including Port LaBelle and the rural lots. Curbside covers everyday household waste, not renovation or cleanout debris. As an agricultural county, many Hendry properties also generate farm, barn and land-clearing debris that needs a dumpster or a facility trip rather than curbside.

## Where C and D and Bulk Debris Go

Construction and demolition debris beyond the curbside limit goes to a transfer station or, more practically for most projects, into a rented roll-off that the hauler takes to the proper facility. The regional disposal infrastructure serving the area includes the Lee/Hendry regional landfill. (cite index="68-1">The Lee/Hendry Regional Solid Waste Disposal Facility is the regional landfill located in neighboring Hendry County.</cite> For a homeowner, the simplest route for any real debris load is a dumpster rather than repeated small curbside set-outs under the county limit.

## Hazardous Waste

As a rural county, Hendry handles household hazardous waste carefully: it never goes in a dumpster or the standard landfill stream. Florida Statute 403.708 restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil, yard trash in some streams and large appliances from standard landfill disposal statewide. For current hazardous-waste options, LaBelle and Hendry County residents should check with Hendry County, since rural counties often use periodic collection events or specific drop-off arrangements rather than a large permanent facility. A dumpster hauler will also advise what must stay out of the box.

## When a Project Needs a Dumpster in Hendry County

Because the curbside construction-debris limit is small, a roll-off earns its place for almost any real project:

- A renovation or home-improvement project over the small curbside limit.
- A roof replacement.
- A mobile-home or manufactured-home cleanout or renovation, common in the area.
- An agricultural, barn or land-clearing cleanup.
- A rental-property turnover or estate cleanout.

For these, a dumpster company serving Hendry County handles delivery and routing. Keep hazardous waste, batteries and appliances out of the box, and describe any agricultural debris to the hauler, since some rural material follows different disposal rules.
