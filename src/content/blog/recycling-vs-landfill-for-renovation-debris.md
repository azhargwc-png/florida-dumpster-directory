# Recycling vs Landfill for Renovation Debris

Some renovation debris is recycled and some is landfilled, and how you load affects the split. This post covers maximizing recycling.

## Maximizing What Gets Recycled

Renovation debris splits into recyclable and non-recyclable:

- **Recyclable (when clean):** concrete and masonry (to aggregate), metal, clean untreated wood, cardboard.
- **Usually landfilled:** mixed debris, treated wood, contaminated or painted materials, general trash, and materials with no recycling stream.

To maximize recycling: keep heavy clean material (concrete) in its own box, keep metal and clean wood separable, avoid contaminating recyclables with trash, and ask the hauler whether they recycle and route to a recycling-capable facility (required in Lee County). The cleaner and more separated your loads, the more is recycled rather than landfilled. For a mixed general load, less is recoverable. Describe your debris to the hauler to plan the loads for recycling.
