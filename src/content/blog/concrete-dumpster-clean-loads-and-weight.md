# Concrete Dumpster: Clean Loads and Weight

Concrete is the material that most tests a dumpster's weight limit, so it goes in a small box, filled partway, as a clean load. This is the pillar guide to concrete disposal: clean loads and weight.

## Clean Loads and Weight

Two rules govern concrete disposal:

**Weight governs, not volume.** Concrete is so dense that a box reaches its weight limit long before it looks full, so concrete goes in a 10-yard box filled only partway, to the weight allowance rather than the rim. A larger box of concrete would be too heavy for the truck to lift safely.

**Keep it a clean load.** A clean concrete load, concrete, block, brick and stone with no wood, trash, rebar-tangled debris or contamination, can be crushed and recycled into aggregate, and is easier to price and handle. Mixing concrete into general debris loses the recycling path and risks an overweight mixed load.

So for anything more than a trace of concrete, use a dedicated small box, fill to the weight allowance, and keep it clean. Describe the job, with rough square footage and thickness, so the hauler sets the right box and allowance.
