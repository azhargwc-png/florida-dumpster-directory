# What Happens If You Overfill or Contaminate a Dumpster?

Two things can turn a routine dumpster rental into an extra charge: overfilling it past the fill line or over the weight limit, and contaminating the load with a banned item. Both are avoidable once you understand them. This post explains what happens in each case and how to stay clear of both.

## Overfilling: The Fill Line

A roll-off has a fill line (usually marked at the top of the walls), and debris must stay level with it, not heaped above it. There are two reasons:

- **Safety and legality.** A truck cannot legally or safely transport a load mounded over the sides, since debris can fall onto the road. An overfilled box may simply not be hauled until you remove the excess.
- **The tarp.** The driver tarps the load for transport, and that requires the debris to sit below the rim.

If a box is overfilled, the hauler may refuse to take it as-is, ask you to remove the excess, or charge an overfill fee. The fix is simple: keep the load level with the fill line, and if you have more debris, arrange a swap or a second box.

## Overweight: The Weight Limit

Separate from volume, each box has a weight allowance (in tons). Heavy materials, concrete, tile, dirt, can exceed the allowance while the box looks only partly full. Going over the weight limit triggers an overage charge, typically billed per ton over. This is why heavy debris goes in a small box filled partway. See [dumpster weight limits by size](/blog/dumpster-weight-limits-by-size/).

## Contamination: Banned Items

A contamination charge applies when a prohibited item turns up in the load, hazardous waste, electronics, tires, batteries, liquids, a refrigerant appliance. The hauler has to pull it out and route it separately, and the landfill may reject a load containing a banned item. The charge covers that extra handling. The fix is to sort out all the banned items before loading; see [what you cannot put in a dumpster](/blog/what-not-to-put-in-a-dumpster/).

## How to Avoid Both

- **Keep the load level with the fill line,** never heaped above it.
- **Use a small box filled partway for heavy material,** so you stay under the weight limit.
- **Sort out banned items before loading,** and take them to the right facility.
- **If you have more debris than fits, arrange a swap or second box** rather than overfilling.
- **Describe your debris to the hauler up front,** so the box size and weight allowance match the job.

A little planning keeps the rental to its quoted price with no surprises.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What happens if I overfill a dumpster?**

**What is a dumpster overage charge?**
An overage charge applies when a load exceeds its weight allowance, which is separate from the volume fill line. Each box has a weight limit in tons, and heavy materials like concrete, tile and dirt can exceed it while the box looks only partly full. Going over triggers a charge, typically billed per ton over the allowance. This is why heavy debris goes in a small box filled partway, and why describing heavy material to the hauler up front, so the allowance fits, avoids a surprise.

**What is a contamination charge?**
A contamination charge applies when a prohibited item turns up in the load, such as hazardous waste, electronics, tires, batteries, liquids or a refrigerant appliance. The hauler has to pull the item out and route it separately, and the landfill may reject a load containing a banned item, so the charge covers that extra handling. The fix is to sort out all banned items before loading and take them to the right facility, keeping the dumpster load clean and avoiding the charge entirely.

**How do I avoid extra dumpster charges?**
Keep the load level with the fill line and never heaped above it; use a small box filled partway for heavy material so you stay under the weight limit; sort out banned items before loading and take them to the right facility; and if you have more debris than fits, arrange a swap or second box rather than overfilling.
