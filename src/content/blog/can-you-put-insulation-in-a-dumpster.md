# Can You Put Insulation and Fiberglass in a Dumpster?

Yes, insulation, fiberglass batts, foam board, and blown-in insulation, goes in a dumpster. It is not hazardous, so the challenge is practical: insulation is light, bulky and messy, and blown-in insulation blows everywhere if loaded loose. The fix is to bag it. This post covers how to dumpster insulation of each type.

## Yes, Insulation Goes In

Fiberglass batts, mineral wool, rigid foam board, spray-foam offcuts and blown-in cellulose or fiberglass are all standard renovation and demolition debris and go in a general roll-off. None is a banned item. Old attic insulation removed during a re-insulation or roof project, and wall insulation from a gut, both go in the box.

## Bag It to Control the Mess

The practical issue is that insulation is light and loose, so it is best bagged:

- **Blown-in insulation** (loose cellulose or fiberglass from an attic) must be bagged, or it blows around the yard and out of the dumpster. Bag it as you remove it, which also keeps the fibers out of the air.
- **Fiberglass batts** compress into bags well and are easier to handle bagged than loose.
- **Wear a mask, gloves and long sleeves,** since fiberglass irritates skin and lungs.
- **Foam board** is rigid, so break it to size; it stacks rather than bagging.

Bagged, insulation packs reasonably and stays put. Loose, it is the kind of light debris that escapes a dumpster in the wind.

## It Is Light, So Volume Governs

Insulation weighs almost nothing, so it never pushes a load toward its weight limit. What it does is take up volume, especially blown-in insulation from a large attic, which is surprisingly voluminous once removed. So size the box for volume, and remember that a big attic's worth of bagged insulation fills more space than you might expect.

**How do I dispose of blown-in attic insulation?**
Bag it as you remove it. **Is fiberglass insulation hazardous?**
Fiberglass insulation is not classified as hazardous waste, so it goes in a regular construction-debris dumpster, not a hazardous-waste facility. However, it is a skin, eye and lung irritant, so wear a mask, gloves and long sleeves when handling it, and bag it to keep the fibers contained. The disposal itself is straightforward; the precautions are about handling it comfortably and safely, not about any disposal restriction. Old insulation in a very old home should be checked if asbestos is suspected.

**Does insulation take up a lot of dumpster space?**
Yes, relative to its weight. Insulation weighs almost nothing, so it never pushes a load toward its weight limit, but it takes up real volume, especially blown-in insulation from a large attic, which expands to fill a lot of space once removed and bagged. So size the box for volume rather than weight when insulation is a big part of the load. Bagging compresses it somewhat and keeps it from escaping, but a full attic's worth still fills more space than expected.
