# 20-Yard Dumpster: Dimensions and What Fits

The 20-yard dumpster holds about 20 cubic yards, roughly six to eight pickup-truck loads. This post covers its dimensions, what fits, and the projects it suits.

## Dimensions and Capacity

A 20-yard roll-off holds about 20 cubic yards, roughly six to eight pickup-truck loads. Exact dimensions vary by hauler, but a typical 20-yard box is around 20 to 22 feet long, 8 feet wide and 4 to 4.5 feet high. See the [20-yard dumpster page](/20-yard-dumpster-rental/).

## What the 20-Yard Is Best For

The 20-yard is the most popular and versatile size, the default for most renovation and cleanout jobs. It holds a lot of general debris while staying low enough to load easily, which is why it is the go-to box when the debris is light-to-moderate and the job is more than a single room.

Typical jobs for a 20-yard include a whole-house cleanout, a shingle roof replacement, a large room or multi-room renovation, a flooring tear-out across a home, or a sizable deck or fence removal.

## Weight Still Matters

Whatever the size, a dumpster has a weight limit as well as a volume limit. The 20-yard is sized for volume, so it suits light-to-moderate debris; heavy material like concrete, tile and dirt still goes in a small 10-yard box filled partway, because it would overload a larger box long before filling it. Describe your debris to the hauler so the size and weight allowance match the job.

## How to Be Sure It Is the Right Size

The safest way to size a box is to describe the project to the hauler: the type of job, the rooms or square footage involved, and the kind of debris. A box too small means a second haul or an overfill problem; a box too large means paying for space you do not use. The [size calculator](/tools/dumpster-size-calculator/) is a good starting point, and the hauler confirms from there.
