# Hurricane Debris vs a Private Dumpster: When to Rent One in Southwest Florida

After a storm in Southwest Florida, there are two ways to get debris off your property: the county's curbside storm-debris collection, and a private dumpster. They are for different kinds of debris, and using the wrong one wastes money or leaves debris sitting. This guide explains which is which, when to rent a dumpster, and how to separate debris the way the counties require, so your cleanup actually gets picked up.

## The Two Systems, and Why It Matters

After a declared hurricane, Southwest Florida counties run a **temporary curbside storm-debris program**: crews make passes along public roads collecting eligible, correctly separated debris placed at the curb, usually at no direct cost to the resident. This is for the storm-generated debris a normal collection cannot handle, vegetative debris and certain storm-damaged materials.

A **private dumpster** is what you rent and fill yourself, for debris the curbside program will not take: ineligible material, debris on private property that crews will not enter to collect, and the rebuild and renovation debris from gutting and repairing a damaged home.

Using the free county program when it applies saves money. Using a private dumpster for everything else keeps your rebuild moving instead of waiting on collection passes. Knowing which debris goes where is the whole trick.

## Use the County Curbside Program When It Applies

The county storm-debris program is the right route when:

- The debris is **eligible storm debris** (typically vegetative debris, and certain storm-damaged building materials the county specifies).
- It can be placed at the **curb on the public right-of-way** for collection.
- It is **separated into the required categories** (see below).
- The program is **actively running** for your area after a declared event.

Counties are specific about this. Charlotte County's hurricane guidance, for example, calls for separating debris into distinct piles and not stacking it toward the house from the sidewalk, and advises against major cutting during an active watch or warning. Sarasota County's disaster-debris rules under Code 106-47 similarly require separation by type. Follow your county's current instructions, because debris that is not separated correctly, or is placed wrong, may be skipped.

## Rent a Private Dumpster When the Debris Is...

A private dumpster is the right route when:

- The debris is **not eligible** for curbside storm collection.
- It is on **private property** that collection crews will not enter, such as a back yard, a pool enclosure, or behind a gate.
- It is **rebuild and renovation debris** from gutting and restoring a damaged home: drywall, flooring, cabinetry, insulation, and so on.
- Your cleanup **cannot wait** for the county's collection schedule, which can run slowly after a major storm.
- You need to clear a **private commercial site.**

In short: the curbside program handles what it can reach at the curb, and the dumpster handles everything on your property and everything from the rebuild.

## How to Separate Storm Debris

Counties typically ask residents to separate curbside storm debris into categories, which commonly include:

- **Vegetative debris:** tree limbs, branches, palm fronds, leaves.
- **Construction and demolition debris:** damaged building materials, drywall, lumber, roofing.
- **Appliances and white goods:** refrigerators, washers, water heaters.
- **Electronics:** TVs, computers.
- **Household hazardous waste:** paint, oil, batteries, which go to a county collection point, never a dumpster or a mixed pile.

Keeping these in separate piles at the curb, not mixed and not blocking the road or drainage, is what lets the county crews collect them. A mixed pile can be passed over. For your private dumpster, the same logic applies in reverse: keep heavy debris (tile, concrete) in its own partly-filled box and lighter rebuild debris in another, and keep hazardous items out entirely.

## Do Not Compete With the Official Program

A key rule: do not use a private dumpster for debris that the county curbside program will collect for free and that qualifies. Pulling eligible vegetative debris into a paid dumpster just costs you money the county would have saved you. The dumpster is for the ineligible, private-property and rebuild debris, the material the free program was never going to take. Use each system for what it is built for.

## Keep Hazardous Waste Out of Both

Storm cleanup turns up paint, motor oil, pool chemicals, propane tanks, batteries and electronics. None of these go in a private dumpster, and they do not go in a mixed curbside pile either. They go to the county household hazardous waste program: Lee County's Topaz Court facility, Charlotte County's Mini-Transfer HHW, Sarasota County's Chemical Collection Centers, and the Collier and DeSoto equivalents. Florida Statute 403.708 restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil and large appliances from standard landfill disposal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I rent a dumpster or wait for county storm pickup after a hurricane?**
Use both, for different debris. Wait for the county curbside program for eligible, separated storm debris you can place at the curb, since it is usually free. Rent a private dumpster for debris the curbside program will not take: ineligible material, debris on private property crews will not enter, and the rebuild debris from gutting and repairing your home. The free program handles what it can reach at the curb, and the dumpster handles the rest.

**Can I put hurricane debris in a regular rented dumpster?**
You can put rebuild and renovation debris, and ineligible or private-property storm debris, in a private dumpster, and that is exactly what it is for. Do not put household hazardous waste in it, paint, oil, batteries, propane and electronics go to the county's household hazardous waste program instead. And do not pay to put eligible vegetative debris in a dumpster when the county curbside program would collect it for free. Match the debris to the right route.

**How do I separate storm debris for county pickup?**
Counties typically ask you to place debris at the curb in separate piles by category: vegetative debris, construction and demolition debris, appliances, electronics, and household hazardous waste, with hazardous items going to a separate collection point rather than a curb pile. Do not mix the piles, block the road, or stack debris toward the house from the sidewalk. Follow your county's current instructions after a declared event, since a mixed or wrongly placed pile can be skipped.

**Why shouldn't I put storm debris in the right-of-way myself?**
For curbside collection, the county tells you exactly how and where to place separated debris, so follow that rather than improvising. Separately, placing a private rented dumpster in the right-of-way or on a swale usually requires a permit from the city or county, because it is public land and a heavy box can block drainage. Keep a rented dumpster on your own driveway unless you have a right-of-way permit, and use the curbside program for curb debris.
