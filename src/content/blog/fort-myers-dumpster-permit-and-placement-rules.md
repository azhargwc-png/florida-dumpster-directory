# Fort Myers Dumpster Permit and Placement Rules

Renting a dumpster in Fort Myers, FL means knowing who governs placement, where a box can sit, and where your debris goes. This guide covers the permit question, placement, and the local Lee County facilities.

## Who Governs Placement in Fort Myers

Fort Myers is an incorporated city and the Lee County seat, so the City of Fort Myers governs its streets and rights-of-way. A box on a city street or right-of-way typically needs a city permit; a box on your own driveway generally needs none.

## Placement and Local Factors

Fort Myers ranges from historic downtown neighborhoods with narrower lots to newer suburban and gated communities, so placement and access vary by area. Check an HOA's rules in a deed-restricted community.

**On your own driveway:** a box entirely on your private driveway usually needs no permit, which is the simplest option. Protect a paver or finished driveway with boards under the roll-off's contact points.

**On the street or right-of-way:** a box there follows the rules of whoever owns the road, so confirm the current requirement before placing one.

## Where Your Debris Goes

Fort Myers is home to the Lee County Topaz Court facility at 6441 Topaz Court, which takes household hazardous waste and electronics free for residents. The county's mandatory C and D recycling applies. Keep hazardous waste, electronics and appliances out of the dumpster.

**Where does hazardous waste go in Fort Myers?**
Fort Myers is home to the Lee County Topaz Court facility at 6441 Topaz Court, which takes household hazardous waste and electronics free for residents, so it is conveniently located for city residents. It accepts paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, oil, batteries and more, and runs a Reuse Center. None of these go in a dumpster, so set them aside before loading a roll-off for a Fort Myers renovation or cleanout.

**What size dumpster do I need in Fort Myers?**
It depends on the job and the debris weight. A cleanout or shingle re-roof usually fits a 20-yard box, a bathroom remodel a 15-yard, and a whole-home renovation a 20- or 30-yard. Heavy debris like concrete or tile goes in a smaller 10-yard box filled partway, since weight hits the limit before the box looks full.
