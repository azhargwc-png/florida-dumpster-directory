# Lee County Construction Debris Recycling Rules

Lee County stands out among Southwest Florida counties for requiring construction and demolition debris to be recycled, not just landfilled. If you are renovating, building or demolishing in Lee County, that rule shapes how your debris is handled. This post explains the requirement, what it means in practice for a homeowner or contractor, and how renting a dumpster keeps the project compliant.

## The Recycling Requirement

Lee County's construction-and-demolition recycling is backed by county ordinance, not just a guideline. (cite index="71-1">Lee Ordinance 07-25 establishes mandatory recycling of commercial and multi-family residential solid waste and construction and demolition debris.</cite> The county also references a mandatory C and D requirement directly in its solid waste materials. (cite index="59-1">Lee County has a mandatory construction and demolition debris recycling requirement.</cite>

To support it, the county runs dedicated facilities. (cite index="67-1">Lee County's facilities include a Construction and Demolition Debris Recycling Facility and a Yard Waste Processing Operation.</cite> So the system is built to divert C and D material into recycling rather than sending it straight to disposal.

## What C and D Debris Is

Construction and demolition debris is the material a building project generates: lumber, drywall, roofing, flooring, cabinetry, concrete, masonry, fixtures and similar. It is distinct from household garbage and from yard waste, and Lee County asks that these streams be kept separate. During storm cleanup the county is explicit about not mixing them. (cite index="57-1">Lee County asks residents to keep separate piles for yard debris and construction and demolition debris including furniture, appliances and electronics, and not to place bagged yard waste in debris piles.</cite>

## What the Rule Means for Your Project

For a homeowner or contractor, the requirement does not usually mean extra paperwork on a small job. It means the debris needs to flow through recycling-capable channels. In practice:

- **A dumpster rental company serving Lee County routes C and D loads** to the county's recycling facility or an approved processor, so renting a roll-off handles the compliance step for you.
- **Keeping loads clean helps.** A load of mostly one material (clean concrete, clean wood) recycles more easily than a mixed load, which is why haulers sometimes advise separating heavy clean debris like concrete into its own box.
- **Hazardous items and white goods stay separate.** Appliances, electronics and hazardous waste are not C and D debris and follow their own routes to Topaz Court.

## How a Dumpster Handles It

The hauler delivers the box, you load the construction debris, and the hauler takes it to a recycling-capable facility. You keep hazardous waste, electronics and appliances out of the box and route them separately. For a concrete-heavy job, ask about a dedicated clean-concrete box, since clean loads recycle better and manage weight.
