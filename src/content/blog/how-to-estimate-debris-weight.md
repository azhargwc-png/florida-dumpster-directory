# How to Estimate Debris Weight

You do not need an exact figure, but a rough sense of whether your debris is light or heavy helps size the box and avoid an overage. This post covers estimating.

## Estimating Light vs Heavy

The practical goal is to know whether your debris is light-and-bulky or heavy-and-dense, which decides whether volume or weight governs:

- **Light and bulky** (furniture, wood, drywall, roofing shingles, packaging, cardboard, carpet): fills volume first, so size by the yards. Weight is rarely the issue.
- **Heavy and dense** (concrete, brick, tile, dirt, sand, stone, wet materials): hits the weight limit first, so size by weight with a small box filled partway.
- **Mixed:** the heavy fraction drives the weight, so separate it into a small box.

You do not need to calculate tonnage; the hauler does that from a description. What helps is telling them the materials and rough amounts, especially any heavy material, and flagging wet debris (flood carpet, soaked drywall) which is far heavier than dry. From that, they set the right box and weight allowance. When in doubt, describe it and let the hauler estimate.
