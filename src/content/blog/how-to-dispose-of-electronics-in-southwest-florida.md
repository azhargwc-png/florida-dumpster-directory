# How to Dispose of Electronics in Southwest Florida

Electronics, computers, TVs, monitors, printers, phones, stay out of a rental dumpster and go through e-waste recycling, which both recovers materials and protects any data on the device. This is the guide to where e-waste goes in each Southwest Florida county and how to handle data security. Clear your electronics before a dumpster job and keep the load clean.

## Why Electronics Go Through Recycling

Electronic devices contain recoverable metals and components plus small amounts of hazardous materials, so they are handled as a separate e-waste stream rather than landfilled. The recyclers also handle data: Lee County notes that its contracted recyclers erase or destroy hard drives and memory-containing devices and break each item down to recover reusable or recyclable components. So e-waste recycling both recovers materials and secures your data, which matters for computers and phones.

## By County

**Lee County.** Set electronics at the curb with trash, or take them to the Household Chemical and Electronics Recycling Facility at Topaz Court, 6441 Topaz Court, Fort Myers.

**Charlotte County.** The Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities in Port Charlotte (19765 Kenilworth Blvd) and Englewood (7070 Environmental Way) handle e-waste.

**Collier County.** The recycling drop-off centers (Tim Nance/Northeast, Naples, Marco Island) take electronics alongside household hazardous waste.

**Sarasota County.** The Chemical Collection Centers, North at 8750 Bee Ridge Road and South at 250 S. Jackson Road, Venice, accept electronics year-round.

**DeSoto and Hendry counties.** Check with the county for current e-waste options, which may be periodic events or specific drop-off arrangements.

## Protecting Your Data

Before recycling a computer, phone or any device that stored data, protect yourself:

- **Back up anything you want to keep.**
- **Sign out of accounts and remove the device** from your account lists.
- **Wipe the device** (factory reset) or remove the drive if you prefer to keep or destroy it yourself.
- Even so, reputable e-waste recyclers erase or destroy data-bearing drives as part of their process, as Lee County's do.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do I recycle electronics in Southwest Florida?**
At your county e-waste route. Lee County lets you set electronics at the curb with trash or take them to the Household Chemical and Electronics Recycling Facility at Topaz Court. Charlotte County takes e-waste at its Mini-Transfer facilities in Port Charlotte and Englewood, Collier County at its recycling drop-off centers, and Sarasota County at the Chemical Collection Centers year-round. DeSoto and Hendry use periodic events or specific arrangements. None of it goes in a dumpster, so clear electronics before a dumpster job.

**Is electronics recycling free in Southwest Florida?**
The county programs accept household electronics from residents as part of their recycling and hazardous-waste services, which are provided for residents (some require proof of residency). This is the no-cost alternative to putting e-waste in the trash or a dumpster. Retailers and private recyclers sometimes offer e-waste take-back too, occasionally for a fee on certain items. For ordinary household devices, the county route is the straightforward free option for residents.

**How do I protect my data when recycling a computer?**
Back up anything you want to keep, sign out of your accounts and remove the device from your account lists, then wipe it with a factory reset, or remove the drive if you prefer to keep or destroy it yourself. Reputable e-waste recyclers also erase or destroy data-bearing drives as part of their process; Lee County's contracted recyclers, for example, erase or destroy hard drives and memory-containing devices. Taking both steps, wiping it yourself and using a proper recycler, gives the best protection.

**Can electronics go in the dumpster for a cleanout?**
No, electronics stay out of a dumpster and go through e-waste recycling instead. For a cleanout or office clear-out, set the computers, TVs, monitors, printers and phones aside and make one e-waste trip to your county facility, which recovers the materials and handles data on storage devices. Keeping e-waste out of the dumpster also avoids a contamination issue and keeps the load clean for the general debris that the dumpster is meant for.
