# 30-Yard Dumpster: Dimensions and What Fits

The 30-yard dumpster holds about 30 cubic yards, roughly nine to twelve pickup-truck loads. This post covers its dimensions, what fits, and the projects it suits.

## Dimensions and Capacity

A 30-yard roll-off holds about 30 cubic yards, roughly nine to twelve pickup-truck loads. Exact dimensions vary by hauler, but a typical 30-yard box is around 20 to 22 feet long, 8 feet wide and 6 feet high. See the [30-yard dumpster page](/30-yard-dumpster-rental/).

## What the 30-Yard Is Best For

The 30-yard has the same footprint as a 20-yard but taller walls, so it holds half again as much. It is the choice for big jobs with high volume: a full renovation, a large demolition, or a rebuild. The taller walls mean debris is tossed up higher, so it suits bulky construction debris rather than heavy material.

Typical jobs for a 30-yard include a whole-home renovation or gut, a large estate cleanout, new construction debris, a major demolition, or a storm rebuild.

## Weight Still Matters

Whatever the size, a dumpster has a weight limit as well as a volume limit. The 30-yard is sized for volume, so it suits light-to-moderate debris; heavy material like concrete, tile and dirt still goes in a small 10-yard box filled partway, because it would overload a larger box long before filling it. Describe your debris to the hauler so the size and weight allowance match the job.

## How to Be Sure It Is the Right Size

The safest way to size a box is to describe the project to the hauler: the type of job, the rooms or square footage involved, and the kind of debris. A box too small means a second haul or an overfill problem; a box too large means paying for space you do not use. The [size calculator](/tools/dumpster-size-calculator/) is a good starting point, and the hauler confirms from there.
