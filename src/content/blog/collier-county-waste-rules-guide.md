# Collier County Waste Rules: Drop-Off Centers, the Landfill and When to Rent a Dumpster

Collier County runs an extensive solid-waste system with multiple recycling drop-off centers, a landfill, and the Immokalee Transfer Station. Knowing how they work tells you what curbside covers, where construction debris and hazardous waste go, and when a project needs a dumpster. This guide covers the drop-off centers, the landfill, free household hazardous waste disposal, and the point where a renovation or cleanout moves to a rented roll-off. It applies across Collier County, including Naples, Marco Island and Immokalee.

## Curbside Collection

Collier County provides frequent curbside service through contracted haulers. (cite index="98-1">Solid Waste Curbside Collection and Disposal Services include twice-a-week curbside collection of household waste and once-a-week recycling collection.</cite> The contracted haulers cover the county outside the City of Naples. (cite index="92-1">The contracted haulers service all areas within the county with the exception of the City of Naples.</cite> Curbside covers routine garbage, recycling and yard waste, not renovation or cleanout debris.

## The Recycling Drop-Off Centers

Collier County operates several recycling drop-off centers that double as household hazardous waste collection points. (cite index="99-1">Collier County operates several recycling drop-off centers to collect and properly dispose of household hazardous waste, and items that can be recycled may also be dropped off at these centers.</cite> Current center locations include Naples, Marco Island, and the Tim Nance (Northeast) center. (cite index="110-1">Drop-off centers include the Tim Nance Center at 9950 Goodlette-Frank Rd, Naples; a Naples center at 2640 Corporate Flight Dr; and Marco Island at 990 Chalmer Dr.</cite>

## The Landfill and Immokalee Transfer Station

Construction and demolition material and yard waste go to the landfill or the transfer station rather than a drop-off center. (cite index="94-1">Commercial generated debris should be taken to the Collier County Landfill or the Immokalee Transfer Station.</cite> (cite index="104-1">The Collier County Landfill is located at 3730 White Lake Boulevard in Naples, open to the public Monday through Saturday.</cite> (cite index="105-1">The Immokalee Transfer Station is at 700 Stockade Rd., Immokalee.</cite> Residents can take a small amount of C and D or yard waste to a drop-off center for a modest per-visit charge, but larger loads go to the landfill or transfer station. For most homeowners, a dumpster hauler handles this routing.

## Household Hazardous Waste

Household hazardous waste never goes in a dumpster, and in Collier County it is accepted free at the recycling drop-off centers. (cite index="94-1">Residents may bring their household hazardous products to any county-operated recycling drop-off center for free disposal.</cite> (cite index="101-1">Residents can drop off household chemicals such as oils, paints, fluorescent bulbs, electronics, herbicides and pesticides.</cite> There is also a dedicated hazardous materials collection center at the landfill. (cite index="105-1">The Collier County Landfill and Hazardous Materials Collection Center is at 3730 White Lake Blvd.</cite>

Florida Statute 403.708 restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil, yard trash in some streams and large appliances from standard landfill disposal, so these stay out of a roll-off.

## When a Project Needs a Dumpster in Collier County

A roll-off earns its place when a project produces more debris than curbside will take, or debris curbside will not take:

- A kitchen, bath or whole-home renovation.
- A roof replacement, very often tile in Collier County.
- A garage, estate or whole-house cleanout.
- A construction or demolition project (C and D goes to the landfill or transfer station).
- A large landscaping tear-out.

For these, a dumpster company serving Collier County handles delivery, hauling and routing the load to the landfill or transfer station. Keep hazardous waste, electronics and appliances out and take them to a drop-off center.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do I take household hazardous waste in Collier County?**
Household hazardous waste is accepted free at any Collier County recycling drop-off center, including the Tim Nance (Northeast) center at 9950 Goodlette-Frank Rd, the Naples center at 2640 Corporate Flight Dr, and Marco Island at 990 Chalmer Dr, plus the Hazardous Materials Collection Center at the landfill, 3730 White Lake Blvd. They take oils, paints, fluorescent bulbs, electronics, herbicides and pesticides. None of these go in a dumpster, so separate them before loading a roll-off for a renovation or cleanout.

**Where does construction debris go in Collier County?**
Construction and demolition material goes to the Collier County Landfill at 3730 White Lake Boulevard, Naples, or the Immokalee Transfer Station at 700 Stockade Rd. Residents can take a small amount of C and D or yard waste to a Naples or Marco Island drop-off center for a modest per-visit charge, but larger loads go to the landfill or transfer station. For a renovation, build or demolition, a dumpster rental company serving Collier County handles this routing, so renting a roll-off is the practical route for most projects.

**Does Collier County collect curbside garbage in Naples?**
The county's contracted haulers service all areas of Collier County except the City of Naples, which handles its own collection. Elsewhere in the county, curbside service includes twice-a-week household waste collection and once-a-week recycling. For a renovation or cleanout anywhere in the county, including within Naples, curbside does not cover the debris, so a rented dumpster is the right tool. Confirm your specific collection arrangement based on whether you are inside the City of Naples or in the broader county.

**What size dumpster do I need for a Collier County project?**
It depends on the job and the debris weight. A cleanout or shingle re-roof usually fits a 20-yard box, a bathroom remodel a 15-yard, and a whole-home renovation a 20- or 30-yard. Collier County's many tile roofs are heavy, so a tile tear-off, concrete or masonry goes in a smaller 10-yard box filled partway. Confirm the size by weight with a dumpster company serving Collier County, and keep hazardous waste, electronics and appliances out, routing them to a drop-off center.
