# Can You Put Appliances in a Dumpster?

Appliances split into two groups for dumpster purposes: those with refrigerant and those without. A washer, dryer, oven or dishwasher is usually fine; a refrigerator, freezer or air conditioner needs the refrigerant recovered first and is handled separately. This post covers which appliances go in, which do not, and how Florida's white-goods rule applies.

## Appliances Without Refrigerant: Usually Fine

Many large appliances have no refrigerant and can go in a roll-off as part of a general load or a cleanout, subject to the hauler's policy: washers and dryers, ovens and ranges, dishwashers, microwaves and water heaters. They are heavy metal, so they add weight, but they are not hazardous. Confirm with the hauler, since some treat all large appliances (white goods) as special items under Florida's landfill rules.

## Appliances With Refrigerant: Recovery First

Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners, dehumidifiers and wine coolers contain refrigerant that must be professionally recovered and certified before disposal. Because of that, many haulers will take one of these only after the refrigerant is removed, or will not take it in the box at all and will direct you to a proper drop-off. Never put a sealed refrigerant appliance in a dumpster without clearing it with the hauler first.

## Florida's White-Goods Rule

Large appliances, called white goods, are restricted from standard landfill disposal under Florida Statute 403.708, along with lead-acid batteries and used oil. In practice this means white goods are handled through a metal-recycling or appliance stream rather than buried, and your county solid-waste program has a route for them. During storm cleanup, counties like Lee are explicit that white goods go curbside but separated from other debris, not mixed into a pile. A dumpster hauler will tell you how they handle appliances for your area.

## The Practical Approach

Plan appliances before delivery:

- **No refrigerant (washer, dryer, oven, dishwasher, water heater):** usually goes in the box; confirm the hauler's white-goods policy.
- **Has refrigerant (fridge, freezer, AC, dehumidifier):** arrange refrigerant recovery, or route to an appliance drop-off; do not put it in the box unprompted.
- **Either way:** mention appliances when you book, since they are heavy and some are special items.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I put a washer or dryer in a dumpster?**
Usually yes. Washers, dryers, ovens, dishwashers, microwaves and water heaters have no refrigerant, so they can go in a roll-off as part of a general load or cleanout, subject to the hauler's policy. They are heavy metal and add weight, but they are not hazardous. Confirm with the hauler, since some treat all large appliances (white goods) as special items under Florida's landfill rules and prefer them kept separate or handled through a metal-recycling stream.

**Can I throw a refrigerator or freezer in a dumpster?**
Not without clearing it first. Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners, dehumidifiers and wine coolers contain refrigerant that must be professionally recovered and certified before disposal. Many haulers will take one only after the refrigerant is removed, or will direct you to a proper appliance drop-off rather than the box. Never put a sealed refrigerant appliance in a dumpster without asking the hauler, since improper disposal of refrigerant is both unsafe and against disposal rules.

**What are white goods and how are they disposed of?**
White goods are large household appliances, like refrigerators, freezers, washers, dryers, ovens and water heaters. Under Florida Statute 403.708, white goods are restricted from standard landfill disposal, so they are handled through a metal-recycling or appliance stream rather than buried. Your county solid-waste program has a route for them, and during storm cleanup counties typically ask that white goods go curbside but separated from other debris. A dumpster hauler will tell you how they handle appliances in your area.

**Do I need to tell the hauler about appliances?**
Yes, mention them when you book. Appliances are heavy, so they affect the weight of your load, and some are special items: anything with refrigerant needs recovery first, and white goods may need to be kept separate under Florida's landfill rules. Telling the hauler up front means they quote the right box and weight allowance, flag anything that cannot go in, and advise on refrigerant recovery or an appliance drop-off if needed, so there are no surprises on pickup day.
