# Collier County Storm Debris Collection

After a declared storm, Collier County coordinates disaster debris removal, and it works best when residents separate their debris correctly. Knowing the rules keeps your piles eligible and tells you when a private dumpster is the right tool for rebuild work. This post covers the county's disaster debris program, the separation approach, and where a rented roll-off fits.

## How Disaster Debris Removal Works

Collier County's Solid and Hazardous Waste Management Division includes disaster debris removal in its services. (cite index="92-1">The Collier County Solid and Hazardous Waste Management Division's services and facilities include disaster debris removal.</cite> After a declared event, the county coordinates collection of eligible storm debris from the right-of-way, separate from routine curbside service, to handle the surge normal collection cannot absorb.

As with the rest of Southwest Florida, the program runs for a limited window after the storm, and eligibility depends on how you set the debris out.

## Separate Your Debris

Eligibility depends on keeping material separated into distinct piles at the right-of-way: vegetative (yard) debris in one, and construction and demolition debris in another, with hazardous waste, appliances and electronics kept out of the general piles. Hazardous waste always goes to a drop-off center, never into a storm pile. Collier County's normal rule already routes C and D to the landfill or transfer station. (cite index="94-1">Commercial generated debris should be taken to the Collier County Landfill or the Immokalee Transfer Station.</cite>

## When a Private Dumpster Is the Right Tool

The county's disaster debris program handles eligible material set at the right-of-way. A private dumpster is the right tool for:

- **Private-property debris** you are clearing yourself rather than setting at the curb.
- **Rebuild and repair debris** from fixing storm damage, which is construction debris.
- **Commercial cleanups** beyond residential scope.
- **Debris you cannot wait on,** when you want it gone from your property now.

A private dumpster never competes with official collection. Use the county program for eligible, separated debris at the right-of-way, and a rented roll-off for ineligible, private-property, commercial or rebuild debris. Keep hazardous waste and appliances out of the box, routing them to a Collier County drop-off center.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How does Collier County handle storm debris?**
Collier County's Solid and Hazardous Waste Management Division includes disaster debris removal among its services. After a declared storm, the county coordinates collection of eligible storm debris from the right-of-way, separate from routine curbside collection, to handle the surge. Eligibility depends on separating debris into distinct piles, vegetative in one and construction and demolition in another, with hazardous waste kept out. The program runs for a limited window, so check the county's current notices after an event.

**Should I rent a dumpster or use Collier County storm collection?**
Use both for what each is meant for. The county disaster debris program is for eligible storm debris you set at the right-of-way, separated into proper piles. A private dumpster is for private-property debris, rebuild and repair debris (which is construction debris), commercial cleanups, and anything you want gone without waiting for a county pass. A private dumpster never competes with official collection, so many homeowners use the county program for yard debris and a roll-off for rebuild work.

**Can I put hazardous waste or appliances in storm debris?**
No. Household hazardous waste is never accepted in storm-debris piles and goes to a Collier County recycling drop-off center. Appliances and electronics are kept out of the general storm piles too. If you are using a private dumpster for storm rebuild work, the same applies: keep paint, chemicals, oil, batteries and appliances out of the box, since they contaminate the load and are restricted from landfill disposal under Florida Statute 403.708. Separate these before loading.

**Where does storm rebuild debris go in Collier County?**
Rebuild and repair debris is construction and demolition material, so it goes to the Collier County Landfill at 3730 White Lake Boulevard or the Immokalee Transfer Station at 700 Stockade Rd, not into a curbside storm pile. The simplest route for a rebuild is to rent a roll-off from a dumpster company serving Collier County, which delivers the box and hauls the C and D load to the proper facility. Keep hazardous waste and appliances separate for a drop-off center.
