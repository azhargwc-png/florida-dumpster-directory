# Can You Put Cabinets in a Dumpster?

Yes, kitchen and bathroom cabinets go in a dumpster, and a cabinet tear-out is a core part of any kitchen or bath remodel. The main tips are to break them down so they pack efficiently and to consider donating cabinets that are still in good shape. This post covers how to dumpster cabinets.

## Yes, Cabinets Go In

Old kitchen base and wall cabinets, bathroom vanities, pantry and laundry cabinetry are all standard renovation debris and go in a general roll-off load. They are made of wood, particleboard, MDF or plywood with some hardware, none of it hazardous. A kitchen or bathroom remodel puts the old cabinets straight in the box.

## Break Them Down

Cabinets are boxes, so left whole they trap empty space and waste the dumpster's volume. Break them down as you remove them: take the doors and drawers off, knock the carcasses apart or cut them down, and flatten the pieces. Broken-down cabinet material packs tightly; whole cabinets stacked in the box leave big air gaps. This matters most in a kitchen remodel, where there can be a dozen or more cabinets plus countertops.

## Countertops Are a Separate Weight Question

The cabinets themselves are light-to-moderate, but what sat on them may not be. A granite, quartz or stone countertop is heavy, like any stone, and counts significantly toward the weight allowance; a laminate counter is light. If you are removing stone countertops with the cabinets, mention it to the hauler, since the stone drives the weight even though the cabinets drive the volume.

## Donate Usable Cabinets

Cabinets removed in working condition, especially from a cosmetic remodel where they are dated rather than damaged, are often wanted. Building-material reuse stores and some charities take usable cabinets, which keeps them out of the landfill and clears them without using dumpster space. A dumpster is the right tool for damaged, water-swollen or particleboard cabinets that have reached the end, and for the volume of a full gut.
