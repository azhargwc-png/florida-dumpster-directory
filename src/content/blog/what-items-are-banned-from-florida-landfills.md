# What Items Are Banned From Florida Landfills?

Several categories of waste are restricted from standard Florida landfill disposal by law, which is why they cannot go in a rental dumpster, since a dumpster load ultimately goes to a landfill or transfer facility. Knowing the banned list up front saves you from contamination charges and rejected loads. This post is the reference for what is restricted, why, and where each item goes instead.

## The Core Florida Landfill Restrictions

Florida Statute 403.708 restricts certain items from disposal in a lined landfill with general solid waste. The core restricted items are:

- **Lead-acid batteries** (vehicle, boat and equipment batteries).
- **Used oil** (motor oil, and oil-bearing filters).
- **Yard trash** in some streams (handled through yard-waste and mulch programs rather than mixed disposal).
- **White goods** (large appliances like refrigerators, freezers, washers, dryers and water heaters).

Because a dumpster load goes to a landfill or transfer facility, these restricted items cannot go in the box, and a hauler will not accept them.

## Items Excluded for Other Reasons

Beyond the statutory list, other items are kept out of dumpsters because they are hazardous, pressurized, or handled through dedicated recycling:

- **Household hazardous waste:** paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, automotive fluids.
- **Electronics (e-waste):** televisions, computers, monitors.
- **Tires:** banned from landfill and handled through tire recycling.
- **Propane tanks and other pressurized cylinders:** explosion risk.
- **Liquids of any kind.**
- **Medical waste and sharps.**
- **Asbestos:** regulated, requiring licensed abatement.

## Where Each Goes Instead

Each restricted item has a route, detailed in the county waste-rules guides:

- **Hazardous waste, oil, pool chemicals, propane:** your county household hazardous waste program (Lee's Topaz Court, Charlotte's Mini-Transfer facilities, Collier's drop-off centers, Sarasota's Chemical Collection Centers).
- **Electronics:** county e-waste recycling.
- **Tires:** retailers or county facilities.
- **Lead-acid batteries:** auto-parts retailers or county hazardous-waste programs.
- **White goods:** county appliance/metal-recycling route; refrigerant appliances need recovery first.
- **Sharps and medications:** county sharps drop-off and pharmaceutical take-back.
- **Asbestos:** licensed abatement and approved disposal.

## The Practical Takeaway

Before you load a dumpster, sort out everything on the restricted list, since one banned item can trigger a contamination charge or a rejected load. Everything that remains, general construction, renovation, cleanout and household debris, goes in the box. When unsure about a specific item, ask the hauler, who knows exactly what their facility will and will not take.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What items are banned from Florida landfills?**
Florida Statute 403.708 restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil (and oil filters), yard trash in some streams, and white goods (large appliances) from standard landfill disposal. Beyond the statute, dumpsters also exclude household hazardous waste, electronics, tires, propane tanks and other pressurized cylinders, liquids, medical waste and sharps, and asbestos. Because a dumpster load goes to a landfill or transfer facility, none of these can go in the box, and each has its own disposal route through county programs, retailers or licensed handlers.

**Why can't these items go in my dumpster?**
Because a dumpster load ultimately goes to a landfill or transfer facility, and these items are either legally restricted from landfill disposal (lead-acid batteries, used oil, white goods, yard trash in some streams under Florida Statute 403.708) or excluded because they are hazardous, pressurized, recyclable through dedicated streams, or dangerous to crews. Putting a restricted item in the box can trigger a contamination charge or a rejected load, so they are kept out and routed to their proper disposal channels instead.

**Where do I take restricted items in Southwest Florida?**
Each has a route. Hazardous waste, oil, pool chemicals and propane go to your county household hazardous waste program (Lee's Topaz Court, Charlotte's Mini-Transfer facilities, Collier's drop-off centers, Sarasota's Chemical Collection Centers). Electronics go to county e-waste recycling, tires to retailers or county facilities, lead-acid batteries to auto-parts stores or county programs, white goods to the county appliance route (refrigerant recovered first), sharps and medications to dedicated drop-offs, and asbestos to licensed abatement. The county waste-rules guides have the specifics.

**What can I put in the dumpster then?**
Almost everything else. Once you have sorted out the restricted items, general construction and demolition debris (wood, drywall, flooring, roofing, cabinetry), household junk, furniture, carpet, renovation and cleanout debris all go in the box. Heavy materials like concrete and dirt go in a small box filled partway for weight, but they are allowed. The banned list is specific and finite; the dumpster handles the large volume of ordinary debris that remains after you set the restricted items aside.
