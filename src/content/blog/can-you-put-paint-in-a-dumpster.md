# Can You Put Paint in a Dumpster?

Wet or liquid paint cannot go in a rental dumpster. Paint is a liquid and a household hazardous waste, so it contaminates the load and is routed to your county's hazardous-waste program instead. Fully dried latex paint is sometimes treated differently. This post covers the paint rule, how to handle leftovers, and where paint goes in Southwest Florida.

## Wet Paint Is a No

Liquid paint, whether latex or oil-based, cannot go in a roll-off. It is a liquid (and dumpsters take no liquids), it can leak and contaminate the load, and it is classified as household hazardous waste. Oil-based paint, stains, varnishes and solvents are especially clearly hazardous. So leftover cans of usable or wet paint from a renovation go to the county program, not the box.

## Dried Latex Paint

There is one nuance. Fully dried latex (water-based) paint is sometimes handled differently from liquid paint, because once hardened it is no longer a liquid. People dry small amounts of leftover latex by leaving the lid off, or mixing in a hardener or kitty litter, until it is solid. Even so, policies vary, so confirm with your hauler before putting even dried paint in the box. Oil-based paint is not handled this way and always goes to hazardous-waste collection.

## Where Paint Goes in Southwest Florida

Leftover paint goes to your county's household hazardous waste program, free for residents:

- **Lee County:** the Topaz Court facility, which also runs a Reuse Center offering usable latex paint back to residents.
- **Charlotte County:** the Mini-Transfer facilities in Port Charlotte and Englewood.
- **Collier County:** any recycling drop-off center.
- **Sarasota County:** the Chemical Collection Centers in Sarasota and Venice, which supply The Re-Uz-It Shop.

Before buying new paint for a project, it is worth checking a county reuse program, since usable paint is often available free.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I throw paint in a dumpster?**
Wet or liquid paint, no. Paint is a liquid, and dumpsters take no liquids; it can leak and contaminate the load, and it is classified as household hazardous waste. Leftover cans of usable or wet paint go to your county's hazardous-waste program, not the box. Fully dried latex paint is sometimes handled differently, since once hardened it is no longer a liquid, but policies vary, so confirm with your hauler first. Oil-based paint always goes to hazardous-waste collection.

**Can I put dried paint in a dumpster?**
Possibly, for fully dried latex (water-based) paint, since once hardened it is no longer a liquid. People dry small amounts by leaving the lid off or mixing in a hardener or kitty litter until solid. Even so, policies vary between haulers and landfills, so confirm before putting even dried paint in the box. This applies only to latex; oil-based paint, stains and varnishes are hazardous and always go to your county's hazardous-waste program, never a dumpster.

**Where do I take leftover paint in Southwest Florida?**
To your county's household hazardous waste program, free for residents. In Lee County it is the Topaz Court facility, which also runs a Reuse Center offering usable latex paint back to residents. Charlotte County takes it at the Port Charlotte and Englewood Mini-Transfer facilities, Collier County at any recycling drop-off center, and Sarasota County at the Chemical Collection Centers in Sarasota and Venice, which supply The Re-Uz-It Shop. Before buying new, check a county reuse program for free usable paint.

**Is paint considered hazardous waste?**
Yes, liquid paint is a household hazardous waste, especially oil-based paint, stains, varnishes and solvents, which are clearly hazardous and must go to a county program. Latex (water-based) paint is also treated as hazardous when liquid, though fully dried latex is sometimes handled differently since it is no longer a liquid. Either way, wet paint never goes in a dumpster, because it leaks, contaminates the load and is restricted. Route leftover paint to your county's hazardous-waste collection.
