# DeSoto County Landfill and Dumpster Rules

DeSoto County, centered on Arcadia, is the most rural of the Southwest Florida markets, and its waste system is built around the county landfill. Knowing the rules tells you what curbside covers, where construction and demolition debris goes, and when a project needs a dumpster. This guide covers the DeSoto County Landfill, what it accepts, hazardous-waste handling, and the point where an Arcadia-area project moves to a rented roll-off.

## The DeSoto County Landfill

The county's disposal hub is the landfill in Arcadia. (cite index="128-1">The DeSoto County Landfill is at 3268 SW Dishong Avenue, Arcadia, FL 34266.</cite> It keeps broad self-haul hours. (cite index="138-1">The DeSoto County Landfill accepts loads Monday through Saturday from 7:30 to 5:00.</cite> There is also a permitted Class III disposal site for construction and yard debris. (cite index="146-1">DeSoto Recycling and Disposal opened in 2018 at 13620 SE HWY-70 in Arcadia and operates as a permitted Class III landfill regulated by the Florida Department of Environmental Protection, handling landfill, construction debris, yard waste and C and D debris.</cite>

For most homeowners and contractors, a dumpster hauler takes project debris to the appropriate DeSoto facility, so you rarely deal with the landfill directly, though the broad hours make self-haul practical for a small load.

## Curbside and Rural Collection

DeSoto County contracts franchised haulers for residential garbage, with the unincorporated areas following county guidelines and the City of Arcadia running its own solid waste division. (cite index="130-1">As an unincorporated area, residents follow DeSoto County's solid waste guidelines, with the county contracting franchised haulers for weekly household garbage collection, and residents also supported by a curbside recycling program.</cite> (cite index="134-1">The City of Arcadia has its own Solid Waste Division handling residential garbage pickups.</cite> Curbside covers routine garbage and recycling, not renovation or cleanout debris, and many rural DeSoto properties generate agricultural and land-clearing debris that needs a dumpster or landfill trip.

## Hazardous Waste

As a rural county, DeSoto does not run the large permanent hazardous-waste facilities of the coastal counties, so hazardous waste is handled carefully: it never goes in a dumpster or the landfill's standard waste stream. Florida Statute 403.708 restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil, yard trash in some streams and large appliances from standard landfill disposal statewide. For current hazardous-waste options, DeSoto County residents should check with DeSoto County Environmental Services, since rural counties often use periodic collection events or direct residents to specific drop-off arrangements. A dumpster hauler will also advise what must be kept out of the box.

## When a Project Needs a Dumpster in DeSoto County

A roll-off earns its place when a project produces more debris than curbside will take, or debris curbside will not take:

- A renovation or home-improvement project.
- A roof replacement.
- An agricultural, barn or land-clearing cleanup, common on rural DeSoto properties.
- A mobile-home or manufactured-home cleanout or renovation.
- A construction or demolition project.

For these, a dumpster company serving DeSoto County handles delivery and routing to the landfill or Class III site. Keep hazardous waste, batteries and appliances out of the box.
