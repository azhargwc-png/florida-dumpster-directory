# Immokalee Dumpster Permit and Placement Rules

Renting a dumpster in Immokalee, FL means working in an unincorporated agricultural community in Collier County's interior, with county placement rules and a local transfer station for debris. This guide covers permits, placement, rural-lot access, and where your debris goes.

## Who Governs Placement in Immokalee

Immokalee is unincorporated, so Collier County governs placement. A box on a public right-of-way follows county rules; a box on your own property generally needs no permit. Many Immokalee lots, including rural and agricultural properties, have room for private driveway or yard placement, which is the simplest route.

## Rural and Agricultural Lots

Immokalee is the agricultural heart of Collier County, so dumpster jobs here often involve farm, barn and land-clearing work alongside home renovations and mobile-home cleanouts. On a rural or agricultural lot:

- **Confirm truck access,** especially on an unpaved drive that can soften after wet-season rain.
- **Describe agricultural debris to the hauler,** since some rural material follows different disposal rules.
- **Allow room for the roll-off truck** to position on a large or open lot.

## The Immokalee Transfer Station

Immokalee has its own debris facility: the Immokalee Transfer Station at 700 Stockade Rd. Construction and demolition debris and yard waste can go there, which is convenient for the area. Household hazardous waste goes to a Collier County recycling drop-off center. Keep hazardous waste, electronics and appliances out of the dumpster; a hauler routes the general and C and D debris to the transfer station or landfill.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Immokalee?**
A box on your own property in Immokalee generally needs no permit, and many lots here, including rural and agricultural ones, have room for private placement. Immokalee is unincorporated, so Collier County rules apply: a box on a public right-of-way follows county rules and may need a right-of-way permit. Confirm access on a rural or unpaved drive, and keep the box on your own property where possible, which is the simplest route and avoids the permit question.

**Where does debris go in Immokalee?**
Immokalee has its own debris facility, the Immokalee Transfer Station at 700 Stockade Rd, which handles construction and demolition debris and yard waste, convenient for the area. Household hazardous waste goes to a Collier County recycling drop-off center. For a renovation, cleanout or land-clearing job, a dumpster company serving Immokalee delivers the box and routes the general and C and D debris to the transfer station or the Collier County Landfill, so you keep hazardous items separate for a drop-off center.

**Can dumpster trucks reach rural Immokalee properties?**
Usually, but confirm access first. Many Immokalee properties are rural or agricultural with open lots that have plenty of room for a roll-off, but access can be an issue on an unpaved drive, especially after wet-season rain softens it. Confirm the delivery truck's approach with the hauler, make sure the drive is firm enough for a loaded roll-off, and allow room for the truck to position. Describe the property when you book so the hauler plans delivery correctly.

**What size dumpster do I need in Immokalee?**
It depends on the job. A home renovation or cleanout usually fits a 15- or 20-yard box, a mobile-home cleanout a 15- or 20-yard (or a 30-yard for a demolition), and an agricultural or land-clearing cleanup a 20- or 30-yard. Heavy debris like concrete goes in a smaller 10-yard box filled partway. Describe the job, including any agricultural debris, to a dumpster company serving Immokalee so they size the box and set the weight allowance correctly.
