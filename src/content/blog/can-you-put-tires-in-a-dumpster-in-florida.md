# Can You Put Tires in a Dumpster in Florida?

No, tires cannot go in a rental dumpster in Florida. They are banned from landfill disposal and are not accepted in standard roll-off loads, so they have their own disposal routes. This post explains why tires are excluded, where they actually go in Southwest Florida, and how to keep them out of your load.

## Why Tires Are Banned

Whole tires are kept out of landfills because they do not compact, they trap gases and float back to the surface, and they collect water that breeds mosquitoes, a real concern in Florida. For these reasons tires are a restricted waste stream, not accepted in a standard dumpster load, and handled through dedicated tire-recycling channels instead, where they are shredded and reused as crumb rubber, fuel or other products.

## Where Tires Go in Southwest Florida

Tires have several disposal routes in the region:

- **Tire retailers.** When you buy new tires, the shop usually takes the old ones, often for a small per-tire fee folded into the sale.
- **County facilities.** County solid-waste facilities accept tires with limits. Charlotte County residents can recycle a number of passenger tires at the curb per year and take tires to the Mini-Transfer facilities, and Collier County accepts tires at the landfill and Immokalee Transfer Station. Sarasota County community cleanup events accept up to four small vehicle tires per household.
- **Tire-recycling drop-offs.** Some counties and events run dedicated tire collections.

Check your county's current rules and limits, since allowances and locations change.

## Keeping Tires Out of Your Load

If you are doing a garage or property cleanout that includes old tires, set them aside before loading the dumpster. A tire discovered in a roll-off load can trigger a contamination charge, since the hauler has to pull it out and route it separately. Handle tires through a retailer or county facility, and keep the dumpster for the rest of the cleanout.
