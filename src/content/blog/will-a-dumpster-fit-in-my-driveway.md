# Will a Dumpster Fit in My Driveway?

Most driveways can take a roll-off dumpster, since common sizes are about 8 feet wide, but length, overhead clearance and the delivery truck's approach all matter. This post covers how to tell if a dumpster will fit your driveway and how to protect it.

## The Space You Need

A roll-off is about 8 feet wide, so width is rarely the problem on a standard driveway. The things to check are:

- **Length.** A 10-yard needs about 12-14 feet; a 20- or 30-yard about 20-22 feet. Measure your driveway's clear length.
- **Delivery clearance.** The truck needs room to back in and tilt the box off the bed, which requires clear space above (no low branches or wires) and a straight approach.
- **A firm, level surface.** The spot should be firm enough to hold a loaded box and reasonably level.

## Driveway vs Street

Keeping the box on your own driveway is usually simplest and avoids a right-of-way permit. If your driveway cannot fit the box, or the job needs the driveway clear, the alternative is the street or swale, which in most Southwest Florida communities needs a permit (and the swale is county right-of-way, not driveway). So fitting it in the driveway is often the easier route.

## Protect the Surface

A loaded dumpster is heavy, and the delivery truck's wheels add point loads. Protect a paver, stamped-concrete or sealed driveway with boards (two-by-tens or plywood) under the box's contact points and where the truck rolls, which spreads the load and prevents cracking or staining. Ask the hauler to set the box on your boards.

## When in Doubt, Ask

If you are unsure whether the box will fit, describe your driveway to the hauler, length, width, slope, overhead obstructions, and they will tell you which size fits and how they will place it. A smaller box is easier to fit if space is tight.
