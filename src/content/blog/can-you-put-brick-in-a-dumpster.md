# Can You Put Brick in a Dumpster?

Yes, brick, block and masonry go in a dumpster, and like concrete they are weight-governed: dense enough that a box fills its weight limit before it looks full. In Southwest Florida, where concrete block (CMU) construction is the norm, brick and block debris is common. This post covers how to dumpster brick and masonry.

## Yes, But Treat It Like Concrete

Brick, concrete block, pavers and stone are all dense masonry, so they behave like concrete in a dumpster: the weight limit governs, not the volume. A box of brick hits its weight ceiling while looking only partly full. So the rule is the same as concrete, use a small box, usually a 10-yard, and fill it only partway, and keep it a clean masonry load where possible. Mixing more than a little brick into a general renovation load risks pushing that load overweight.

## Clean Masonry Recycles

A clean load of brick, block and concrete (no wood, trash or contamination) can often be crushed and recycled into aggregate, the same as clean concrete. Keeping masonry separate from general debris preserves that recycling path and keeps the heavy material from overloading a mixed box. For a demolition that produces both masonry and general debris, plan a heavy box for the masonry and a general box for the rest.

## Common Southwest Florida Masonry Jobs

Block is the standard wall construction here, so masonry debris shows up in wall demolitions, removing a block planter or knee wall, tearing out a brick walkway or paver patio, and chimney or fireplace removal. All are classic small-heavy-box jobs. Describe the volume to the hauler, who sets the weight allowance.
