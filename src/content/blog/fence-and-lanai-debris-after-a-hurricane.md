# Fence and Lanai Debris After a Hurricane

Hurricanes commonly destroy fences and screen enclosures (lanais, pool cages), and the debris, wood, vinyl, and light aluminum, is a frequent post-storm dumpster job. This post covers handling it.

## Handling Fence and Lanai Debris

Fencing and screen enclosures are among the first things a storm takes down in Southwest Florida. Handling the debris:

- **Fence debris** (wood, vinyl, chain-link) is bulky; the concrete post footings add weight if dug out. See the fencing post for details.
- **Lanai and pool-cage aluminum** is light and very bulky; break the frame into sections so it packs rather than fanning out.
- **Screen material** is light and bundles easily.

If the county's curbside storm program is running, eligible fence and enclosure debris set at the right-of-way and separated (C and D pile) may be collected. For debris you are clearing yourself, on a rebuild, or that you cannot wait on, a private dumpster is the right tool. The aluminum fills volume, not weight, so size for bulk; the footings are the only heavy part.
