# How to Choose Between Dumpster Sizes

Choosing a dumpster size comes down to two questions: how much debris, and how heavy? Get those right and the box follows. This post gives a simple method for picking the size, and the one rule of thumb that saves most people from a wrong choice.

## Step 1: How Much Debris (Volume)

Estimate the volume by the scope of the job:

- **A single room or small job:** 10-15 yard.
- **A whole house, a re-roof, or a multi-room renovation:** 20 yard (the versatile default).
- **A whole-home gut, large demolition or rebuild:** 30 yard.
- **A large commercial or construction job:** 40 yard.

The [size calculator](/tools/dumpster-size-calculator/) turns a rough description into an estimate.

## Step 2: How Heavy (Weight)

Then check the debris type, because weight can override volume:

- **Light, bulky debris** (furniture, wood, drywall, roofing shingles, cardboard): volume governs, use the size from Step 1.
- **Heavy, dense debris** (concrete, tile, dirt, brick, stone): weight governs, use a small 10-yard filled partway, regardless of volume.
- **A mix:** often a small heavy box plus a larger light box.

## Step 3: When in Doubt, Size Up

The single most useful rule: when you are between two sizes, choose the larger one. Running out of room mid-project means a second haul or an overfilled box that cannot be collected, which costs more than the modest difference for a slightly larger box. The exception is heavy debris, where you never size up past a 10-yard, because weight, not volume, is the limit.

## Step 4: Describe It to the Hauler

Finally, describe the job to the hauler, the scope, the rooms or square footage, and the debris type. They size the box and set the weight allowance, and they have done it for jobs like yours many times. A two-minute conversation is the best way to land on the right size.
