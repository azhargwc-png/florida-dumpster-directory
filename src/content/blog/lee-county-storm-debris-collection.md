# Lee County Storm Debris Collection

After a declared storm, Lee County runs a curbside storm-debris collection program, and it works only if residents separate their debris correctly. Knowing the rules keeps your piles eligible for county pickup and tells you when a private dumpster is the right tool instead. This post covers how the county collects storm debris, the separation rules, what is not accepted, and where a rented roll-off fits.

## How County Collection Works

When Lee County activates storm-debris collection after an event, its hauler makes passes along the right-of-way to collect eligible material that residents have set out. (cite index="57-1">After recent storms, regular curbside collection for residential garbage, recycling, yard waste and bulk waste resumed once debris collection was completed.</cite> The program is for the surge of storm-generated material that normal weekly collection cannot absorb, and it runs for a limited window after the event.

## The Separation Rules

Eligibility depends on separating debris into distinct piles. Lee County is specific about this. (cite index="57-1">Keep separate piles for yard debris, construction and demolition debris including furniture, appliances and electronics, and do not place bagged yard waste in your debris piles.</cite> The county also sets handling rules for the piles. (cite index="63-1">Make sure that each pile is clear of any electrical lines, water meters, mailboxes and fire hydrants.</cite>

And it draws clear lines on what is excluded. (cite index="63-1">No white goods are accepted mixed in; place white goods curbside next to but separated from regular trash and other debris. No household chemical or hazardous waste is accepted; dispose of it at the Topaz Court facility. No black bags; only clear bags or containerized debris are accepted so debris is visible.</cite>

## What Is Not Accepted Curbside

Pulling the exclusions together, the county storm-debris program will not take, mixed into a pile:

- Household chemical or hazardous waste, which goes to Topaz Court.
- White goods (large appliances), which go curbside but separated.
- Debris in black bags, since it must be visible.
- Yard waste in bags, which must be loose or containerized.

Beyond those, the county also discourages generating new debris mid-crisis. (cite index="63-1">Do not begin construction projects that produce debris unless absolutely necessary to protect life and property.</cite>

## When a Private Dumpster Is the Right Tool

The county program is for eligible storm debris set at the right-of-way. A private dumpster is the right tool for the material the program does not cover:

- **Private-property debris** that you are clearing yourself rather than setting at the curb.
- **Rebuild and repair debris** from fixing storm damage, which is construction debris, not storm debris.
- **Commercial debris** and larger cleanups beyond residential scope.
- **Debris when you cannot wait** for the county pass and want it gone from your property.

A private dumpster never competes with the official curbside collection. Use the county program for eligible, separated debris at the right-of-way, and a rented roll-off for ineligible, private-property, commercial or rebuild debris. Keep hazardous waste and white goods out of the box, the same as the county rules require.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I set out storm debris in Lee County?**
Separate it into distinct piles at the right-of-way: yard debris in one, and construction and demolition debris including furniture, appliances and electronics in another, and do not bag yard waste. Keep each pile clear of electrical lines, water meters, mailboxes and fire hydrants. Do not use black bags, since debris must be visible, and do not mix in household chemical or hazardous waste, which goes to the Topaz Court facility. Following the separation rules keeps your debris eligible for county pickup.

**What storm debris will Lee County not collect curbside?**
Mixed into a pile, the county program will not take household chemical or hazardous waste, which goes to Topaz Court, or white goods, which must be set out separately from other debris. Debris in black bags is not accepted because it must be visible, and yard waste must be loose or containerized rather than bagged. For private-property, rebuild, commercial debris, or anything you cannot wait to have collected, a private dumpster is the right tool instead of the curbside program.

**Should I rent a dumpster or use county storm collection?**
Use both for what each is meant for. The county curbside program is for eligible storm debris you set at the right-of-way, separated into proper piles. A private dumpster is for the material the program does not cover: private-property debris, rebuild and repair debris (which is construction debris), commercial cleanups, and anything you want gone without waiting for the county pass. A private dumpster never competes with official collection, so many homeowners use the county program for yard debris and a roll-off for rebuild work.

**Can I put hazardous waste or appliances in storm debris?**
No. Household chemical and hazardous waste is never accepted in storm-debris piles and goes to the Lee County Topaz Court facility. White goods (large appliances) must be set out curbside but separated from other debris, not mixed into a pile. If you are using a private dumpster for storm rebuild work, the same rules apply: keep hazardous waste and appliances out of the box, since they contaminate the load and are restricted from landfill disposal under Florida Statute 403.708.
