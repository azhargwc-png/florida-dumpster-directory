# Charlotte County Waste Rules: Facilities, Hazardous Waste and When to Rent a Dumpster

Charlotte County runs its solid-waste system through two Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities plus a landfill, and knowing how they work tells you what regular collection handles, what you self-haul, and when a project needs a dumpster. This guide covers the facilities, household hazardous waste, the proof-of-residency rule, and the point where a renovation or cleanout moves to a rented roll-off. It applies across Charlotte County, including Port Charlotte, Punta Gorda, Rotonda West and the Charlotte side of Englewood.

## The Two Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities

Charlotte County's self-haul and recycling hub is a pair of facilities. (cite index="87-1">The Mid-County Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility is at 19765 Kenilworth Blvd., Port Charlotte, and the West Charlotte Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility is at 7070 Environmental Way, Englewood.</cite> These are where residents take recyclables, scrap metal, tires, oversized yard trimmings and household hazardous waste that curbside does not cover. (cite index="90-1">Charlotte County residents with proof of residency can self-haul recyclables, scrap metal, tires, furniture, oversized yard trimmings and household hazardous waste to a Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility.</cite>

Using them requires proof that you live in the county. (cite index="91-1">The Mid-County Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facility is free to use for Charlotte County residents, with proof of residency required, such as a valid driver's license, utility bill or tax bill, and Post Office Box addresses are not accepted.</cite>

The county also runs a reuse program. (cite index="87-1">The Household Reuse Center lets residents pick up household product reuse items free at the Mid-County Mini-Transfer facility.</cite>

## Household Hazardous Waste

Household hazardous waste never goes in a dumpster, and in Charlotte County it goes to the Mini-Transfer facilities. (cite index="76-1">Residents can bring their household hazardous waste to the County's Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities, and a select few items can be picked up by residential curbside service; the county only offers these services for residential households, so businesses must hire a hazardous hauler.</cite> (cite index="84-1">The Charlotte County Solid Waste Division offers year-round household hazardous waste collection at its recycling facilities in Port Charlotte and Englewood, and proof of residency is required.</cite>

The facilities also take sharps, mercury devices and fluorescent bulbs. (cite index="75-1">Households can take their household hazardous waste and used hypodermic needles to the Mini-Transfer Facilities.</cite> (cite index="77-1">Households can recycle mercury devices and fluorescent light bulbs at the Mini-Transfer Facilities, while businesses must secure a hazardous waste hauler.</cite> Used cooking oil has a dedicated drop-off. (cite index="76-1">Charlotte County residents can dispose of used cooking oil free of charge at both Mid-County and West Charlotte Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities.</cite>

## The Landfill

Beyond the Mini-Transfer facilities, Charlotte County operates a landfill for disposal. (cite index="85-1">The Charlotte County Landfill is located on Zemel Road, and the county's Solid Waste Division provides information about it.</cite> For most homeowners, a dumpster hauler takes project debris to the appropriate facility, so you rarely deal with the landfill directly.

## Curbside and the Bulk Limit

Residential curbside service covers routine garbage, recycling and yard waste, but it caps bulk and renovation debris. Third-party summaries of the county's bulk rules note that renovation and home-improvement debris, and loads over about four cubic yards, fall outside curbside bulk pickup, which is exactly where a dumpster comes in. Always confirm the current bulk allowance with the Solid Waste Division, since curbside limits change.

## When a Project Needs a Dumpster in Charlotte County

A roll-off earns its place when a project produces more debris than curbside bulk will take, or debris it will not take:

- A renovation or home-improvement project (outside curbside bulk).
- A roof replacement, shingle or tile.
- A garage, estate or whole-house cleanout.
- A construction or demolition project.
- A storm rebuild, separate from the county's storm-debris program.

For these, a dumpster company serving Charlotte County handles delivery and hauling. Keep hazardous waste, sharps, electronics and appliances out of the box and take them to a Mini-Transfer facility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where are the Charlotte County waste facilities?**
Charlotte County runs two Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities: Mid-County at 19765 Kenilworth Blvd., Port Charlotte, and West Charlotte at 7070 Environmental Way, Englewood. These take recyclables, scrap metal, tires, oversized yard trimmings and household hazardous waste, free for residents with proof of residency, such as a driver's license, utility bill or tax bill (Post Office Box addresses are not accepted). The county also operates a landfill on Zemel Road for disposal, though a dumpster hauler usually handles that routing for your project.

**Where does household hazardous waste go in Charlotte County?**
Household hazardous waste goes to the Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities in Port Charlotte and Englewood, which offer year-round collection for residents with proof of residency. They take paint, chemicals, batteries, used hypodermic needles (sharps), mercury devices, fluorescent bulbs and used cooking oil. The service is residential only, so businesses must hire a hazardous waste hauler. None of these items can go in a dumpster, so separate them before loading a roll-off for a renovation or cleanout.

**Do I need proof of residency to use Charlotte County facilities?**
Yes. The Mini-Transfer and Recycling Facilities are free for Charlotte County residents, but proof of residency is required, such as a valid driver's license, utility bill or tax bill. Post Office Box addresses are not accepted. This applies to self-hauling recyclables, scrap metal, tires, yard trimmings and household hazardous waste. If you are managing a larger renovation or cleanout, a dumpster rental is often simpler than repeated self-haul trips, and the hauler handles the disposal routing.

**What size dumpster do I need for a Charlotte County project?**
It depends on the job and the debris weight. A cleanout or shingle re-roof usually fits a 20-yard box, a bathroom remodel a 15-yard, and a whole-home renovation a 20- or 30-yard. Heavy debris like concrete or tile goes in a smaller 10-yard box filled partway, since weight hits the limit before the box looks full. Confirm the size by weight with a dumpster company serving Charlotte County, and keep hazardous waste, sharps and appliances out, routing them to a Mini-Transfer facility.
