# What Materials Count as Heavy Debris?

Heavy debris is the category that is governed by weight rather than volume, and knowing what counts tells you when to use a small box filled partway. This post lists the heavy materials.

## The Heavy-Debris List

Heavy (dense) debris, which hits the weight limit before the volume, includes:

- **Concrete, block, brick, stone and masonry.**
- **Tile** (ceramic, porcelain and especially stone), including roof tile and floor tile with mortar.
- **Dirt, soil, sand and sod.**
- **Asphalt.**
- **Wet materials:** flood-soaked carpet, drywall, insulation.
- **Plaster and stucco.**
- **Cast iron** (old tubs, pipe).

All of these go in a small box (usually a 10-yard) filled only partway, to the weight allowance, and ideally kept as clean single-material loads for recycling and pricing. By contrast, light-and-bulky debris, furniture, wood, drywall (dry), shingles, packaging, cardboard, carpet (dry), metal, appliances, is governed by volume and sized by the yards. When a job includes any heavy material, separate it so it does not push a general load overweight. Describe heavy debris to the hauler.
