# Can You Put a Hot Tub in a Dumpster?

Yes, a hot tub or spa can go in a dumpster, but almost never in one piece. A hot tub has to be drained and cut into sections to fit, and its mix of acrylic shell, foam insulation, wood or composite cabinet and plumbing makes it bulky and awkward. This post covers how to dumpster a hot tub and what size box you need.

## Yes, But Cut It Up

A whole hot tub is too large and too rigid to drop into a roll-off and too awkward to fill the box efficiently. The standard approach is to cut it into manageable sections with a reciprocating saw or circular saw: remove the cabinet panels, cut the acrylic shell and its foam backing into pieces, and separate the plumbing and equipment. Cut up, the pieces pack into the box; whole, a hot tub wastes most of the space around it.

## Drain It First

A hot tub holds a lot of water, and dumpsters take no liquids, so drain it completely before you start cutting. Use the drain valve or a submersible pump, and direct the water appropriately (not into a storm drain). A drained, cut-up hot tub is just bulky debris; a full one is both a no-liquids violation and far too heavy to move.

## What Size Box

A cut-up hot tub is bulky but not especially heavy once drained, so volume governs. The sections from a typical hot tub, plus any decking or surround you remove with it, often fit a 15- or 20-yard box, especially combined with other project debris. If the hot tub is the whole job, a 10- or 15-yard may do once it is cut down. Describe it to the hauler for a size recommendation.

**How do I cut up a hot tub for disposal?**
Drain it fully first, then work in sections: remove the cabinet side panels, cut the acrylic shell and its foam insulation backing into pieces with a reciprocating or circular saw, and separate the plumbing and the equipment pack. Wear eye protection and a dust mask, since cutting the foam and acrylic is messy. Load the pieces into the dumpster as you go. Cutting it down is what lets a hot tub fit efficiently rather than hogging the whole box.

**Do I need to drain a hot tub before dumping it?**
Yes, completely. A hot tub holds a lot of water, dumpsters take no liquids, and a full tub is far too heavy to move. Drain it using the drain valve or a submersible pump before you start cutting, and direct the water appropriately rather than into a storm drain. Once drained and cut into sections, the hot tub is just bulky debris that loads into the box; undrained, it is both a no-liquids violation and a safety problem to handle.
