# Can You Put Fencing in a Dumpster?

Yes, fence debris goes in a dumpster, and in Southwest Florida fence removal is common after storms and between property updates. The handling depends on the material (wood, vinyl, chain-link) and on the concrete footings that often come up with the posts. This post covers how to dumpster fencing of each type.

## Yes, Fencing Goes In

Removed fence panels, pickets, rails and posts are standard construction and demolition debris and go in a general roll-off. A fence tear-out, a storm-damaged fence, or a replacement project all put the old fencing in the box. It is not a banned item.

## By Material

- **Wood fencing** is clean wood if untreated, or treated lumber if pressure-treated (most outdoor fencing is treated). Either way it goes in the box; see the treated-lumber post for why treated wood stays out of clean-wood recycling.
- **Vinyl (PVC) fencing** is light and bulky; break panels down so they pack flat rather than fanning out.
- **Chain-link fencing** is metal, so it has scrap value; some people set it aside for a scrap-metal recycler, but it can also go in the box.
- **Aluminum or ornamental fencing** is light metal, similar to chain-link for disposal.

## The Concrete Post Footings

The thing people forget: fence posts are often set in concrete footings, and when you dig them out, those footings are heavy concrete chunks. A few footings add real weight, and a whole fence line's worth can push a load toward its limit. Treat the footings like any concrete debris; for a long fence line, mention them to the hauler so the weight allowance accounts for them, or keep heavy concrete footings in a separate heavier load.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I put a removed fence in a dumpster?**
Yes. Fence panels, pickets, rails and posts are standard construction and demolition debris and go in a general roll-off. A fence tear-out, a storm-damaged fence or a replacement project all put the old fencing in the box; it is not a banned item. The handling depends a little on the material, wood, vinyl, chain-link or aluminum, and on the concrete footings that often come up with the posts and add weight. Mention a long fence line to the hauler so the box and allowance fit.

**How do I handle different fence materials?**
Wood fencing goes in the box, though most outdoor fencing is pressure-treated, which stays out of any clean-wood recycling stream. Vinyl (PVC) fencing is light and bulky, so break panels down to pack flat. Chain-link and aluminum fencing are metal with scrap value, so some people set them aside for a scrap-metal recycler, though they can also go in the box. Whatever the material, the fencing itself is non-hazardous C and D debris; the weight concern is the concrete footings.

**What about the concrete fence-post footings?**
They are the heavy part. Fence posts are often set in concrete footings, and when you dig them out those footings are dense concrete chunks. A few add real weight, and a whole fence line's worth can push a load toward its weight limit. Treat them like any concrete debris: for a long fence line, mention them to the hauler so the weight allowance accounts for them, or keep the heavy footings in a separate, smaller heavier-debris box.

**Can I dumpster a storm-damaged fence?**
Yes. Fence sections blown down or broken by a storm are standard debris and go in a dumpster. If you are using a private dumpster for storm cleanup, remember it is for private-property and rebuild debris; eligible storm debris set at the right-of-way may be collected by your county's storm program instead. For fence debris you are clearing yourself, or a replacement project, a rented roll-off is the right tool. Watch the weight if concrete footings come up with the posts.
