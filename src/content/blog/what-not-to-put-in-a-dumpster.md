# What You Cannot Put in a Dumpster in Florida

A roll-off dumpster takes most construction and household debris, but Florida law and hauler rules keep a specific list of items out, including hazardous waste, electronics, tires, batteries, appliances with refrigerant and liquids. Putting a banned item in the box can trigger a contamination charge, delay the haul, or in some cases get the load rejected at the landfill. This post covers what is prohibited, why, and where each item goes instead.

## Why Some Items Are Banned

Two forces set the list. The first is Florida Statute 403.708, which restricts certain items from standard landfill disposal, including lead-acid batteries, used oil, large appliances and yard trash in some streams. The second is the hauler's own rules and the landfill's acceptance policy, which keep out materials that are hazardous, hard to process, or dangerous to crews. The practical result is a consistent list across most Southwest Florida haulers.

## Items Kept Out of the Box

### Hazardous waste
Paint, solvents, gasoline, motor oil, pesticides, pool chemicals, propane tanks and other household hazardous waste cannot go in a roll-off. These contaminate the load and are dangerous to handle. They go to your county's household hazardous waste program instead, such as Lee County's facility on Topaz Court, Charlotte County's Mini-Transfer drop-off, or Sarasota County's Chemical Collection Centers.

### Electronics
Televisions, computers, monitors and other e-waste are kept out of standard loads and are routed to county electronics recycling, which most Southwest Florida counties run alongside their HHW programs.

### Tires
Tires are banned from landfill disposal and are not accepted in standard roll-off loads. Take them to a tire retailer or the county's tire-recycling drop-off.

### Batteries
Lead-acid (vehicle) batteries are restricted under Florida Statute 403.708 and go to a retailer or HHW facility. Rechargeable and lithium batteries also stay out, since they are a fire risk in the box and at the landfill.

### Appliances with refrigerant
Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners and dehumidifiers contain refrigerant that must be professionally recovered before disposal. Many haulers will take an appliance only after the refrigerant is removed and certified, so ask first.

### Liquids
No liquids of any kind, including partial paint cans unless the paint is fully dried, since liquids leak, add weight and can contaminate a load.

### Other commonly restricted items
Medical waste, asbestos, and in some streams yard trash and whole large appliances are also restricted. When in doubt about any item, ask the hauler before it goes in the box.

## What Usually Is Allowed

For contrast, a standard roll-off readily takes construction and demolition debris (wood, drywall, flooring, cabinetry), roofing material, concrete and masonry within the weight allowance, furniture, general household junk, and most renovation debris. The weight-heavy materials, concrete, tile and dirt, are allowed but usually go in a smaller box filled partway so the load stays under the weight limit.

## How to Avoid a Contamination Charge

A contamination charge is what a hauler bills when a banned item turns up in the load, since it has to be sorted out or the load reworked. Avoid it with a few habits:

- **Set aside the banned items before you start loading,** so they never reach the box by accident.
- **Keep a small bin for hazardous items** and take them to the county HHW facility in one trip.
- **Ask the hauler for their full accepted-items list** when you book, since lists vary slightly.
- **Flag any appliance with refrigerant** so it is handled correctly.
- **Never pour liquids into the box,** and let paint dry out fully before disposal.

A quick sort at the start is far cheaper than a contamination charge or a rejected load at the end.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I put paint in a dumpster in Florida?**
Wet paint cannot go in a roll-off, since liquids leak, add weight and contaminate the load. Fully dried latex paint is sometimes accepted, but confirm with the hauler first. Unused or wet paint, solvents and stains go to your county's household hazardous waste program, such as Lee County's Topaz Court facility, Charlotte County's Mini-Transfer drop-off or Sarasota County's Chemical Collection Centers. Letting leftover latex paint dry out fully before disposal is the usual way to handle small amounts.

**Can I throw away a refrigerator or air conditioner?**
Not without handling the refrigerant first. Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners and dehumidifiers contain refrigerant that must be professionally recovered and certified before disposal, so many haulers will take the appliance only after that is done. Ask the hauler about their appliance policy before loading one, since some accept them with proof of refrigerant recovery and others direct you to a different drop-off. This keeps the load compliant and avoids a rejected pickup.

**What happens if I put a banned item in the dumpster?**
If a banned item turns up in the load, the hauler may bill a contamination charge to sort it out or rework the load, delay the pickup, or in some cases have the load rejected at the landfill. The charge exists because the item has to be separated and handled separately. The fix is to set aside hazardous waste, electronics, tires, batteries, refrigerant appliances and liquids before you load, and to ask the hauler for their full accepted-items list when you book.

**Where does household hazardous waste go in Southwest Florida?**
Household hazardous waste goes to your county's HHW program, not a roll-off. Lee County runs a facility on Topaz Court, Charlotte County has a Mini-Transfer drop-off, and Sarasota County operates Chemical Collection Centers, with Collier, DeSoto and Hendry counties running their own programs. These take paint, oil, solvents, pesticides, batteries, propane tanks and electronics. Separating these items before loading a dumpster keeps your load compliant and avoids a contamination charge.
