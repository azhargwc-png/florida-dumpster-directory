# Why Concrete Needs a Separate Dumpster

Putting a little concrete in a general debris box is a common mistake that leads to overweight loads. This post explains why concrete needs its own box.

## The Weight Problem

A dumpster has a weight limit as well as a volume limit, and concrete is dense enough that even a modest amount adds a lot of weight. Put some concrete in a general renovation load and you can quietly push the whole load over its weight allowance, triggering a per-ton overage, often costing more than a second small box would have.

So concrete gets its own box: a 10-yard filled only partway, to the weight allowance. This keeps the concrete load within the truck's safe lifting capacity, keeps the general debris box within its allowance, and preserves the clean concrete for recycling. For a job with both concrete and general debris (a patio tear-out during a renovation, say), that means two boxes, a small heavy one and a larger light one. Describe both to the hauler.
