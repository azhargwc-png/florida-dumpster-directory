# Can You Put Glass in a Dumpster?

Yes, construction and household glass, window panes, mirrors, glass doors and glass debris, goes in a dumpster. It is not hazardous, so the main concern is safety: broken glass is a cut hazard to you and to the crew. This post covers how to dumpster glass safely, including Southwest Florida's impact windows.

## Yes, With Safe Handling

Window glass, mirrors, shower-door glass, glass tabletops and broken glass from a renovation all go in a general roll-off. Glass is not a banned or hazardous item (note that glass bottles and jars are recyclable through curbside, but construction glass is a disposal item). The reason to handle it carefully is cut risk, not any disposal restriction.

## Handle Broken Glass Safely

A few precautions:

- **Contain broken glass** in a sturdy box or thick contractor bag rather than tossing loose shards into the dumpster, so it does not scatter and so the crew is not cut when the box is emptied.
- **Wear gloves and eye protection** when handling and loading it.
- **Label a bag or box of broken glass** if your hauler asks, so crews know to handle it with care.
- **Lay large panes flat or against a wall** of the box, not standing up where they can fall and shatter.

Contained and placed carefully, glass is straightforward; loose shards are the thing to avoid.

## Impact Windows in Southwest Florida

Hurricane impact windows are common here, and a window-replacement project generates their old frames and glass. Impact glass is laminated (two layers bonded to a plastic interlayer), so it tends to crack rather than shatter into loose shards, which actually makes it a bit safer to handle than ordinary glass. The old aluminum or vinyl frames go in with the glass. For a whole-house window replacement, the volume of frames and glass may warrant its own box alongside other project debris.

**Can I dumpster old windows?**
Yes. Old window glass and the aluminum or vinyl frames are standard renovation debris and go in a general roll-off. In Southwest Florida, hurricane impact windows are common; their laminated glass tends to crack rather than shatter into loose shards, so it is a bit safer to handle than ordinary glass. The frames go in with the glass. For a whole-house window replacement, the volume of frames and glass may warrant its own box alongside other project debris.

**Is window glass recyclable?**
Construction and window glass is generally a disposal item rather than a curbside-recycling item, because it differs from the container glass (bottles and jars) that recycling programs accept, and it often comes mixed with frames, glazing and other materials. So old windows and glass debris go in the dumpster for disposal rather than curbside recycling. Household glass bottles and jars, by contrast, go in your curbside recycling, not the construction dumpster. Keep the two streams separate.
