## About Sanibel and Why Dumpster Demand Is Different Here

Sanibel is a barrier island and incorporated city in Lee County, famous for its shelling beaches, conservation ethic and low-key, high-value homes. It is small, affluent and tightly regulated to protect the island's ecology, and since Hurricane Ian it has been one big rebuild zone.

Census ACS figures record Sanibel with a population around 6,400, an owner-occupied housing rate near 90%, and a median owner-occupied home value around $912,200, among the highest in the region, with an older median age in the high 60s. Hurricane Ian made landfall near here in September 2022 and caused catastrophic damage across the island, so Sanibel's dumpster demand right now is dominated by rebuild, restoration and high-end reconstruction, on top of the usual renovation of expensive homes.

Three forces shape dumpster demand on Sanibel:

- **Hurricane Ian rebuild and restoration,** still the dominant activity, from gut-and-restore projects to full rebuilds of lost homes.
- **High-value renovation,** since the island's homes are expensive and owners invest heavily.
- **Strict island environmental and access constraints,** which make placement, scheduling and debris handling more involved than on the mainland.

## The Sanibel Climate and Dumpster Timing

Sanibel has a warm, wet subtropical coastal climate, fully exposed to Gulf storms as a barrier island. The June to November wet season overlaps hurricane season, and Sanibel's exposure is exactly why Ian hit it so hard. That exposure also means rebuild and storm-hardening work is year-round, with demand and island logistics tightening sharply during and after any storm event.

## Storm, Hurricane and Rebuild Debris on Sanibel

Sanibel's recent history is defined by Hurricane Ian, so storm and rebuild debris handling is central here.

After a declared hurricane, Lee County and the City of Sanibel coordinate storm-debris collection for eligible material, with specific separation and placement rules for the island. Use that program for eligible debris when it is running. A private dumpster is for ineligible material, private-property debris, and the gut-and-restore and rebuild debris that makes up most island projects now.

Keep hazardous waste out of the box. Paint, motor oil, batteries, propane tanks and electronics go to Lee County's Topaz Court facility, never a roll-off, and this matters doubly on an ecologically sensitive island. Florida Statute 403.708 restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil and large appliances from standard landfill disposal.
