## About Fort Myers Beach and Why Dumpster Demand Is Different Here

Fort Myers Beach is a town on Estero Island, a barrier island in Lee County, long a tourist and residential beach community of cottages, canal homes, condos and resorts. Hurricane Ian made landfall near here in September 2022 and devastated the island, so its dumpster demand is dominated by rebuild and reconstruction.

Census figures record Fort Myers Beach with a pre-storm population in the 5,000 to 6,000 range and a median owner-occupied home value in the range of roughly $475,000 to $540,000, with an older median age around 66. Much of the island's housing was damaged or destroyed by Ian, and rebuilding, elevating and reconstructing homes has defined the island since. That makes storm rebuild, demolition and high-value reconstruction the core of dumpster demand, on top of normal renovation.

Two forces shape dumpster demand on Fort Myers Beach:

- **Hurricane Ian rebuild,** still the dominant activity, from demolition of destroyed structures to full reconstruction and elevation.

## Placement, Access and Town Rules on Fort Myers Beach

Fort Myers Beach is an incorporated town with its own rules, and island logistics make placement involved.

**Island access.** Everything reaches the island over the Matanzas Pass bridge, so delivery scheduling can be tighter than on the mainland. Book ahead and confirm the hauler's island turnaround.

**Town of Fort Myers Beach rules.** The town regulates placement, duration and construction debris, and during the rebuild there may be specific permitting. A box on a street or public right-of-way typically needs a town or county permit; a box on your own lot usually does not. Confirm current requirements with the town for your project.

**Tight island lots.** Island lots are often small, and a developed or rebuilding lot may have limited placement space. A smaller box sometimes fits where a longer one will not. Confirm access before booking.

**Driveway and site protection.** Protect driveways and manage the site to keep debris out of the island's beaches and waterways.

## Storm, Hurricane and Rebuild Debris on Fort Myers Beach

Fort Myers Beach's recent history is defined by Hurricane Ian, so storm and rebuild debris handling is central. After a declared hurricane, Lee County and the town coordinate storm-debris collection for eligible material with specific island rules; use that program for eligible debris when it is running. A private dumpster is for ineligible material, private-property debris, and the demolition and rebuild debris that makes up most island projects now. Keep hazardous waste out of the box, since this is a sensitive coastal environment; take paint, oil, batteries, propane and electronics to Lee County's Topaz Court facility.
