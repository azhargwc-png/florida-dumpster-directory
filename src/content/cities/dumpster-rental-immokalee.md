## About Immokalee and Why Dumpster Demand Is Different Here

Immokalee is an unincorporated community in inland northeastern Collier County, about 40 miles northeast of Naples, and it is the agricultural heart of the county, a major center for farm work, packing and related industry. Its makeup is very different from coastal Collier, which shapes dumpster demand.

Census ACS 2019 to 2023 figures record the Immokalee area with a population in the 25,000 to 30,000 range, an owner-occupied housing rate around 39%, which is low and reflects a large rental and farmworker-housing share, and a median owner-occupied home value of $197,000, well below the coastal county figures, with a young median age around 30. The housing mix includes modest single-family homes, rental housing, farmworker housing and mobile homes. That profile means dumpster jobs here skew toward rental turnover, repair and renovation of modest and older homes, mobile-home work, and agricultural and property cleanups, rather than high-value coastal remodels.

Two forces shape the local demand:

- **Rental and farmworker-housing turnover,** where landlord cleanouts, repairs and unit renovations are a steady source of debris given the large rental share.

## Placement and Permits in Immokalee

Immokalee is unincorporated Collier County, so county right-of-way rules apply.

**On your own lot:** a box entirely on your private property usually needs no permit, and many Immokalee properties have room for private placement.

**On the street or right-of-way:** a box on a public right-of-way in unincorporated Collier County usually needs a county right-of-way permit.

**Rental and mobile-home communities:** in rental housing, farmworker housing or a mobile-home community, the property owner or management often controls placement, so clear the spot before booking.

**Rural-lot access:** rural and agricultural properties usually have room, but confirm the approach is firm enough for a loaded roll-off truck, especially on an unpaved drive after wet-season rain.
