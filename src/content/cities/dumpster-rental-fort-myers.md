## About Fort Myers and Why Dumpsters Stay Busy Here

Fort Myers is the county seat of Lee County and the historic urban center of Southwest Florida, with a revitalized downtown river district, historic neighborhoods and a wide mix of housing from old homes to new apartments. Its makeup is different from the owner-heavy coastal communities, which shapes its dumpster demand.

Census ACS 2019 to 2023 figures record the City of Fort Myers with a population around 92,000, an owner-occupied housing rate of 49.4%, which is low for the region and reflects a large rental and multifamily share, and a median owner-occupied home value of $351,600, with a median age near 40. That combination, a dense urban city with significant rental housing, historic neighborhoods, and ongoing downtown development, produces a steady mix of renovation, rental turnover, commercial build-out and construction work.

Three forces keep local haulers working:

- **A large rental and multifamily base,** where tenant turnover, unit renovations and landlord cleanouts are constant in a city where about half of occupied homes are renter-occupied.
- **Historic neighborhoods and a redeveloping downtown,** where owners and developers invest in substantial remodels and build-outs.
- **Construction and storm rebuild,** since Fort Myers keeps growing and was hard hit by Hurricane Ian in 2022.

## The Fort Myers Climate and Dumpster Timing

Fort Myers has a warm, wet subtropical climate. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals at the Fort Myers, Page Field station (USW00012835) record an annual mean near 74.7°F and about 54 inches of rain, concentrated in the June to November wet season that overlaps hurricane season. ## Placement and Permits in Fort Myers

Fort Myers is where city and county jurisdiction can both apply, so confirming which governs your address matters.

**Inside the City of Fort Myers limits:** city rules and permitting apply, and a box on a city street or right-of-way typically needs a city permit. A box entirely on your private property usually does not.

**In unincorporated Lee County around the city:** county right-of-way rules apply, and a box on the public right-of-way typically needs a county permit.

**Dense and historic-neighborhood access:** the downtown river district and historic neighborhoods like Dean Park and the Edison Park area have tighter lots and streets than the sprawling suburbs. Confirm the delivery truck has a clear approach and overhead clearance, and on a tight lot a smaller box may be the only thing that fits.

**Driveway protection:** protect the brick, paver and sealed driveways found in older Fort Myers neighborhoods with boards under the contact points.

Because the city-versus-county question turns on your exact address, confirm with the hauler which permit, if any, your placement needs.

## Lee County Construction and Disposal Rules

For construction and build-out work, the Lee County rules apply. Covered construction-and-demolition projects face a 50% recycling-or-reuse requirement under county ordinance, with a certification form, which affects contractors and developers pulling permits in Fort Myers. A hauler who works local construction will know the approved facilities and how to meet the requirement. Household hazardous waste from any job goes to Lee County's Topaz Court facility, never a roll-off.

## Storm and Hurricane Debris in Fort Myers

Fort Myers was hit hard by Hurricane Ian in 2022, and storm debris follows different rules from a normal dumpster job. Use the Lee County curbside program for eligible, separated storm debris when it is running after a declared event, and a private dumpster for ineligible material, private-property debris and rebuild work. Keep household hazardous waste out and take it to Lee County's Topaz Court facility. Florida Statute 403.708 restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil and large appliances from standard landfill disposal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Fort Myers?**
It depends on where the box sits and whether your address is inside the City of Fort Myers or in unincorporated Lee County. A dumpster on your own private driveway usually needs no permit in either jurisdiction. A box on a city street or right-of-way typically needs a City of Fort Myers permit, and a box on a county right-of-way typically needs a county permit. Confirm which applies to your address with the hauler before booking.

**What size dumpster do I need in Fort Myers?**
Most Fort Myers home projects fit a 20-yard box, the standard for shingle re-roofs, remodels and cleanouts. Tile roofs, concrete and masonry go in a 10-yard box filled partway, since dense material hits the weight limit before the box looks full. Rental turnovers often fit a 15- or 20-yard, and commercial build-outs a 30- or 40-yard. Confirm the size by weight with the hauler, especially for tile.

**Will a dumpster fit in a historic Fort Myers neighborhood?**
It can, but access is tighter than in the suburbs. The downtown river district and historic neighborhoods like Dean Park have smaller lots and narrower streets, so confirm the delivery truck has a clear approach and overhead clearance before booking a long box. On a tight lot, a 10- or 15-yard box may be the only size that fits. Protect brick, paver or sealed driveways, common in these older areas, with boards under the contact points.

**Does Lee County require construction debris recycling in Fort Myers?**
Yes. Covered construction-and-demolition projects in Lee County face a 50% recycling-or-reuse requirement under county ordinance, with a certification form, which applies to qualifying projects and build-outs in Fort Myers. A hauler who works local construction will know which approved facilities handle the load and how to meet the requirement. Separating clean materials like concrete and wood makes it easier to meet and can lower disposal cost.

**Where does household hazardous waste go in Fort Myers?**
Household hazardous waste cannot go in a roll-off. In Fort Myers, paint, motor oil, solvents, pesticides, batteries, propane tanks and electronics go to Lee County's Topaz Court household hazardous waste facility. Florida Statute 403.708 also restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil and large appliances from standard landfill disposal. Separate these items before loading a dumpster, and ask the hauler for their full accepted-items list to avoid a contamination charge.
