## Placement, Swales and the Two-County Question in Englewood

Englewood is unincorporated and straddles a county line, so placement involves the swale rule and the two-county question.

**Which county?** Because Englewood sits in both Sarasota and Charlotte counties, which county handles permits, landfills and debris rules depends on where your property is. Confirm which county you are in, since the two counties run different programs.

**On your own driveway:** a box entirely on your private driveway usually needs no permit.

**On the street or swale:** Englewood's streets are largely swale-lined, and a swale is county right-of-way, not an extension of your driveway, so a box there usually needs a right-of-way permit from the applicable county. Placing a heavy box on a swale without one can draw a fine and damage the drainage grade. Keep the box on your driveway unless you pull the permit.

**Mobile-home communities.** For work in one of Englewood's mobile-home or manufactured-home parks, the park or community owner also controls placement, so clear the spot before booking.

**Driveway protection.** Protect paver or sealed driveways with boards under the roll-off's contact points.

## Storm and Hurricane Debris in Englewood

Englewood is fully exposed to Gulf storms, and storm debris follows different rules from a normal dumpster job. Use the applicable county's curbside storm-debris program, Sarasota or Charlotte, for eligible, separated debris when it is running after a declared event, following the county's guidance on separating debris and keeping it clear of the house. Use a private dumpster for ineligible material, private-property debris and rebuild work. Keep household hazardous waste out and take it to the applicable county's program, Sarasota County's Chemical Collection Centers or Charlotte County's Mini-Transfer drop-off. Florida Statute 403.708 restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil and large appliances from standard landfill disposal.
