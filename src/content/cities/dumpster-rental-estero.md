## About Estero and Why Dumpsters Stay Busy Here

Estero is an incorporated village in Lee County, between Fort Myers and Bonita Springs, known for its master-planned communities, golf and gated neighborhoods. It is affluent and skews older, and much of its housing sits inside homeowner associations, which shapes how dumpster jobs work here.

Census ACS 2019 to 2023 figures record Estero with a population around 37,000, an owner-occupied housing rate of 84.2%, and a median owner-occupied home value of $430,500, with a median age in the mid-60s. The village has grown rapidly, so its housing stock is relatively new, much of it built from the 2000s on, but the high ownership rate and older base mean steady remodeling, renovation of now-maturing homes, and move-related cleanouts.

Three forces keep local haulers working:

## Placement, Permits and HOAs in Estero

Estero is an incorporated village in Lee County, so Village of Estero rules apply inside the village and Lee County rules apply in the unincorporated edges. But in Estero, the homeowner association is often the first rule to check.

**On your own driveway:** a box entirely on your private driveway usually needs no government permit.

**On the street or right-of-way:** a box on a public right-of-way typically needs a permit from the village or county that owns the road.

**HOA rules come first in many Estero communities.** Gated and master-planned neighborhoods frequently have their own covenants on where a dumpster can sit, how long it can stay, and sometimes requiring advance notice or approval. Check your HOA's rules before you book, because an association can require you to move or screen a box regardless of what the village allows. A hauler who works Estero communities will be familiar with common HOA expectations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Estero?**
A dumpster entirely on your own driveway in Estero usually needs no government permit. A box on a street or public right-of-way typically needs a permit from the Village of Estero or Lee County, depending on who owns the road. Just as important in Estero, your homeowner association often has its own rules on dumpster placement and duration, so check the HOA before you book, since an association can require you to move or screen the box.

**Do Estero HOAs have rules about dumpsters?**
Many do. Estero's gated and master-planned communities frequently have covenants governing where a dumpster can sit, how long it can stay, and sometimes requiring advance notice or approval before delivery. These HOA rules often apply on top of, and can be stricter than, the village and county rules. Check with your association before booking, and choose a hauler familiar with how Estero communities handle dumpster placement and screening.

**What size dumpster do I need in Estero?**
Most Estero projects fit a 20-yard box, the standard for remodels, shingle re-roofs and cleanouts. Tile roofs, concrete and masonry go in a 10-yard box filled partway, since dense material hits the weight limit before the box looks full. Full gut jobs may need a 30-yard, and a lanai or pool-cage tear-down fits a 20- or 30-yard since the aluminum is light. Confirm the size by weight with the hauler.

**What size dumpster for a lanai or pool-cage tear-down in Estero?**
A lanai, pool cage or screen enclosure is mostly aluminum framing and screen, bulky but light, so a 20- or 30-yard box sized by volume usually fits, and weight is rarely the limit. This work is common in Estero's communities and spikes after storms damage enclosures. If the tear-down also includes a concrete deck or footers, keep that heavy material in a separate 10-yard box filled partway.

**Where does household hazardous waste go in Estero?**
Household hazardous waste cannot go in a roll-off. In Estero, paint, motor oil, solvents, pesticides, batteries, propane tanks and electronics go to Lee County's Topaz Court household hazardous waste facility. Florida Statute 403.708 also restricts lead-acid batteries, used oil and large appliances from standard landfill disposal. Separate these items before loading a dumpster, and ask the hauler for their full accepted-items list.
