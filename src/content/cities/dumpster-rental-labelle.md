## About LaBelle and Why Dumpster Demand Is Different Here

LaBelle is the county seat of Hendry County, an inland agricultural community on the Caloosahatchee River east of Fort Myers, known for cattle, citrus and its small-town character. It is more affordable and more rural than the coastal cities, which shapes dumpster demand.

Census ACS 2019 to 2023 figures record the City of LaBelle with a population around 5,200, an owner-occupied housing rate of 72.5%, and a median owner-occupied home value of $139,300, among the lowest in Southwest Florida, with a young median age near 38. The broader LaBelle area, including Port LaBelle, adds rural lots, mobile homes and agricultural properties. That profile means dumpster jobs here skew toward repair and renovation of modest and older homes, mobile-home work, rental turnover, and agricultural and property cleanups, rather than high-value coastal remodels.

Two forces shape the local demand:

- **Modest-home and rental turnover,** where repairs, renovations and landlord cleanouts are a steady source of debris.
- **Agricultural, rural-property and mobile-home work,** from barn and land cleanups to manufactured-home renovation and replacement.

## Hendry County Construction-Debris Limit

This is where LaBelle has a specific local rule worth knowing. In Hendry County, residents may set out a limited amount of construction debris at the curb, up to about 2 cubic yards per month, with larger amounts going to the transfer station or a rented dumpster. So for anything beyond a very small repair, a dumpster is usually the practical route, since the curbside allowance is small. A local hauler will know the current limit and the transfer-station options. LaBelle's commercial enclosure rules generally exempt open-top roll-offs, but confirm the current code for a commercial job.

## Placement and Permits in LaBelle

LaBelle is the Hendry County seat, so City of LaBelle rules apply inside the city and Hendry County rules apply in the unincorporated areas, including Port LaBelle and rural lots.

**On your own lot:** a box entirely on your private property usually needs no permit, and many LaBelle and rural Hendry lots have room for private placement.

**On the street or right-of-way:** a box on a public right-of-way typically needs a permit from the city or county that owns the road.
