## What a 30-Yard Dumpster Holds

A 30-yard roll-off typically measures about 22 feet long, 8 feet wide and 6 feet tall. The extra height, about two feet more than a 20-yard box, is where the added capacity lives, and it changes how you load: you are lifting debris higher, and most 30-yard jobs involve a crew or a contractor rather than a homeowner loading by hand. At roughly nine to twelve pickup-truck loads, it carries the debris from a gut renovation or a new-construction phase without constant swaps.

## When the 30-Yard Is the Right Call

The 30 suits high-volume, lower-density debris: a whole-home interior gut (drywall, cabinetry, flooring, trim from every room), new construction and framing debris, large additions, roof-and-structure demolition, and big commercial-adjacent cleanouts. If the debris is bulky but light, the 30's volume is the advantage and the weight limit rarely binds.

It is the wrong box for dense debris. Concrete, tile, brick and dirt in a 30-yard container will blow past the weight allowance when the box is a fraction full, those loads belong in a [10-yard](/10-yard-dumpster-rental/) partial fill. And for a standard single-family re-roof or a two-to-three-room remodel, a [20-yard](/20-yard-dumpster-rental/) is usually enough; the 30 is more box than those jobs need.

## Weight Limits

A 30-yard box commonly includes a weight allowance of roughly 3 to 4 tons, set by the hauler, confirm the figure and the per-ton overage rate when you call. Because the 30 is chosen for bulky, light debris, volume is usually the binding limit. The risk is the reverse of the small box: people fill a big container with heavier-than-expected debris and get an overage surprise, so if your gut job includes a lot of tile, plaster or masonry, flag it to the hauler and consider splitting heavy material into a separate smaller box.

## Placement and Permits

A 30-yard box needs the same footprint as a 20, about 22 feet of straight run, but its height and the weight of a full load make placement and driveway protection more important. A loaded 30 is heavy; on pavers, sealed concrete or an older driveway, insist the hauler protects the surface. The permit rule is unchanged: own driveway, usually none; street, sidewalk or swale, usually a right-of-way permit from the city or county. On a new-construction site, confirm whether the general contractor or the property owner is responsible for the permit.

## What to Look for in a Company Running 30-Yard Dumpsters

For a big job, the swap-out plan matters: ask whether the hauler can pull a full 30 and drop an empty one promptly so the project does not stall. Confirm the weight allowance and overage rate, and be honest about how much heavy debris is in the mix. Confirm the debris destination, a new-construction or demolition load is construction-and-demolition waste and, in Lee County, covered C&D projects face a 50% recycling-or-reuse requirement.
