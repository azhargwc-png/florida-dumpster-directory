This page breaks down exactly what a 20-yard box is, what it holds, where its weight limit bites, which jobs it suits, and how to place it. If your debris is dense, like concrete or clay tile, read the weight section closely, because a 20 is often the wrong choice for heavy loads.

## What a 20-Yard Dumpster Holds

A 20-yard roll-off is a mid-size container that balances capacity against ease of loading.

### Dimensions

- Length: about 22 feet
- Width: about 8 feet
- Height: about 4 to 4.5 feet

Exact dimensions vary by hauler and container brand, so confirm them if space is tight. The low wall height is the 20-yard box's signature feature. You can lift debris over the side, and most of these containers have a swinging rear barn door so you can walk heavy items in rather than heaving everything up and over a tall wall.

### Capacity in plain terms

- About 20 cubic yards of debris
- Roughly six to eight full-size pickup-truck loads
- Enough for a typical single-family roof replacement, a two-to-three-room remodel, or a full garage-and-attic cleanout

This is the broad middle of residential projects, which is why haulers reach for the 20 when a homeowner describes a job and is not sure what to order.

## Weight Limits, Where the 20-Yard Bites

Volume is only half the story, and in Southwest Florida it is often the less important half.

### How the weight allowance works

Every roll-off includes a weight allowance measured in tons. Go over it and you pay an overage charge billed per ton. A 20-yard box commonly includes somewhere in the range of 2 to 3 tons, but the exact figure is set by the individual hauler, so confirm it when you call.

### The heavy-debris trap

Southwest Florida's densest debris fills a box on weight long before it fills it on volume. A 20-yard container loaded with any of these will hit its weight limit when it looks about a third full:

- Clean concrete, block and brick
- Clay or concrete roof tile
- Dirt, sand and sod
- Wet, sand-laden demolition debris

Load one of those into a 20 and you pay overage on a container that appears mostly empty. That is why local roofers tearing off tile, and homeowners removing concrete or dirt, are routinely steered into a [10-yard](/10-yard-dumpster-rental/) box filled only partway. See the [concrete and heavy debris page](/concrete-dumpster-rental/) for how that works.

### What the 20-yard is actually built for

For light, bulky debris, the 20 fills on volume first and the weight allowance rarely binds. That is the load it is designed to carry:

- Asphalt shingles, but not tile
- Drywall, cabinetry and trim
- Flooring and carpet
- Furniture and general cleanout clutter
- Framing offcuts and light construction waste

## Best Uses for a 20-Yard Dumpster in Southwest Florida

The 20-yard roll-off suits the jobs that come up most often across the region.

### Shingle roof replacements

A typical asphalt-shingle re-roof on a single-family home produces debris that fits a 20-yard box well on both volume and weight. Tile roofs are the exception, because tile is three to four times heavier than shingles and needs a smaller box sized by weight. See the [roofing page](/roofing-dumpster-rental/).

### Whole-home and multi-room remodels

Kitchen-and-bath projects, flooring replacement across several rooms, and interior refresh jobs generate bulky but relatively light debris that the 20 handles comfortably.

### Estate and whole-house cleanouts

The region's large retiree and snowbird population means estate cleanouts and move-outs are steady work. A 20-yard box holds the furniture, boxes and accumulated household goods from a typical home.

### Lanai, pool-cage and screen-enclosure tear-downs

Common after storms, where the aluminum framing and screen are bulky but light, so volume is the limit and the 20 fits well.

## Placement and Permits for a 20-Yard Box

Where the box can sit decides both access and whether you need a permit.

### Space the box needs

- A firm, level run about 22 feet long for the container
- Roughly 60 feet of straight clearance for the delivery truck to approach and tilt the box off
- Overhead space clear of wires and low branches

A standard Southwest Florida driveway usually handles this without trouble.

### The permit rule

- Entirely on your own driveway: usually no permit
- On a street, sidewalk, swale or other public right-of-way: usually a right-of-way or obstruction permit from the city or county that owns the road

In swale-heavy communities like Port Charlotte, Cape Coral and Lehigh Acres, the grassy curb strip is a drainage swale and counts as right-of-way, not driveway.

### Protecting your driveway

A loaded 20-yard box concentrates several tons on small wheels and contact points. On paver, sealed or older concrete driveways, which are common in Southwest Florida's newer communities, that can crack or stain the surface. Ask the hauler to put plywood or boards under the wheels and contact points before delivery.

## What to Look for in a Company Running 20-Yard Dumpsters

Before you book, confirm each of these:

- **Weight allowance and overage rate.** Ask for the included tonnage and the per-ton overage charge. This is the number that turns a predictable job into a surprise bill.
- **Rental period.** Confirm how many days are included and what a longer hold costs.
- **Mid-job swap.** On a big remodel or re-roof, ask whether they can swap a full 20 for an empty one so you do not have to order a larger box than you need.
- **Debris destination.** Confirm they haul to a facility that accepts your debris type, since construction debris, clean concrete, yard waste and household hazardous waste all follow different routes.
- **Permit responsibility.** If the box will not sit entirely on your driveway, confirm whether they or you pull the right-of-way permit.
- **Driveway protection.** Ask how they protect a paver or sealed driveway.
- **Track record.** Use the Google review counts in the directory listings as one signal, alongside the phone call.

**What is the weight limit on a 20-yard dumpster?**

**Can I put concrete or tile in a 20-yard dumpster?**
You can, but it is usually the wrong choice. Concrete, brick and clay tile are so dense that they hit a 20-yard box's weight limit when it looks only a third full, leaving you paying overage on a mostly empty container. **How much space do I need for a 20-yard dumpster?**

A standard Southwest Florida driveway usually accommodates this. If the box will sit anywhere off your private property, you will likely need a right-of-way permit from the city or county.

**Will a 20-yard dumpster crack my driveway?**
