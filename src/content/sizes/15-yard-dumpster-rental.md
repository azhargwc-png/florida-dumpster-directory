# 15-Yard Dumpster Rental in Southwest Florida, The In-Between Box

The 15-yard roll-off is the size that bridges the gap between the small heavy-debris 10 and the all-purpose 20. It holds about 15 cubic yards, roughly five to six pickup-truck loads, in a mid-length box that suits jobs too big for a 10 but not quite needing a 20. Not every Southwest Florida hauler stocks a 15, so it is worth asking; where it is available, it is a tidy fit for flooring tear-outs, small shingle roofs and single-car garage cleanouts. ## What a 15-Yard Dumpster Holds

A 15-yard roll-off typically measures about 16 feet long, 8 feet wide and 4 feet tall. The capacity, around 15 cubic yards, or five to six pickup-truck loads, sits neatly between the 10 and the 20, and so does the footprint, which helps on medium lots where a 20-yard box is slightly more than the driveway wants.

## When the 15-Yard Is the Right Call

The 15 earns its place on jobs where a 10-yard box would mean a second haul but a 20-yard box would ship half-empty. Think a full flooring tear-out across two or three rooms, a small-to-mid shingle roof, a single-car garage plus attic cleanout, or a bathroom-and-a-half remodel. It also handles a mix of moderate-weight debris, flooring, drywall, cabinetry, without reaching a weight limit as fast as the dense-debris 10.

For heavy debris like concrete or tile, the 15 carries the same warning as the 20: dense material hits the weight allowance before the box fills, so a [10-yard](/10-yard-dumpster-rental/) partial fill is usually better. For a large cleanout or a full re-roof, step up to the [20-yard](/20-yard-dumpster-rental/).

## Weight Limits

A 15-yard box commonly includes a weight allowance of roughly 2 to 3 tons, set by the hauler, confirm the figure and the per-ton overage rate when you call. For the moderate-weight remodel debris the 15 is built for, volume is usually the binding limit, not weight. For anything dense, ask the hauler to size by weight.

## Placement and Permits

A 15-yard box needs a firm, level run of about 16 feet plus truck approach room. The permit rule holds across every size: your own driveway, usually no permit; a street, sidewalk or swale, usually a right-of-way permit from the city or county. Protect a paver or sealed driveway with boards under the contact points.

First, confirm they actually stock a 15, many Southwest Florida haulers run only 10, 20, 30 and 40-yard boxes, in which case they will point you to the nearest size. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a 15-yard dumpster a common size?**
The 15-yard is a real but less universal size, many Southwest Florida haulers stock 10, 20, 30 and 40-yard boxes and skip the 15. Where it is offered, it fills the gap between the 10 and the 20 for jobs like flooring tear-outs and small roofs. If a company does not run a 15, ask whether a 10 with a second haul or a 20 better fits your debris.

**What jobs suit a 15-yard dumpster?**
A 15-yard box fits jobs too big for a 10 but not large enough to justify a 20: a multi-room flooring tear-out, a small-to-mid shingle roof, a single-car garage-and-attic cleanout, or a bathroom-and-a-half remodel. It carries moderate-weight remodel debris well, where volume rather than weight is usually the limit. For heavy or large loads, a different size fits better.

**What is the weight limit on a 15-yard dumpster?**
A 15-yard box commonly includes a weight allowance of about 2 to 3 tons, set by the individual hauler, so confirm it and the per-ton overage rate when you call. For the moderate remodel debris the size is built for, you will usually fill the box on volume before reaching the weight limit. Dense debris like concrete or tile is the exception and belongs in a smaller box.

**Can I put a mix of debris in a 15-yard dumpster?**
Yes, mixed remodel debris, flooring, drywall, cabinetry, trim and general construction waste, is exactly what a 15-yard box handles well. Keep out prohibited items like household hazardous waste, tires, batteries and appliances with refrigerant, which follow separate Florida disposal rules. Ask the hauler for their accepted-items list, since a contaminated load can mean a surcharge or a refused pickup.
