Demolition debris is mixed and often heavy, which makes sizing a demolition dumpster more of a judgment call than any other job. A shed tear-down, an interior gut, a pool-cage or lanai removal and a full structure demolition each produce a different debris profile, and the dense material inside them, plaster, tile, masonry, is what decides the box. ## Sizing a Demolition Dumpster by Job Type

**Interior gut jobs**, stripping a home or a set of rooms down to the studs, produce high volumes of drywall, flooring, cabinetry and trim, mostly light but bulky. A single-room gut may fit a [15-](/15-yard-dumpster-rental/) or [20-yard](/20-yard-dumpster-rental/) box; a whole-home gut usually needs a [30-yard](/30-yard-dumpster-rental/) or scheduled swaps. Watch for tile and plaster, which add weight fast.

**Lanai, pool-cage and screen-enclosure tear-downs**, common across Southwest Florida and frequent after storms, are mostly aluminum framing and screen: bulky but very light, so volume is the limit and a 20- or 30-yard box suits them.

**Shed and small-structure demolition** varies with the construction: a wood shed is light and bulky; a block or masonry shed is heavy and belongs in a smaller box sized by weight.

**Full structure demolition** is usually a contractor job with heavy mixed debris, often requiring multiple boxes or a large container paired with a separate heavy-debris box for concrete and block.

## Mixed Demolition Debris and Weight

The hard part of demolition sizing is that one job produces both light bulky material and dense heavy material. A gut job that includes tile floors, plaster walls or a masonry feature can run over a big box's weight limit even though most of the debris is light drywall and wood. The fix is to separate: keep the heavy material, tile, plaster, concrete, block, in a partly-filled [10-yard](/10-yard-dumpster-rental/) box, and the light bulky material in a larger box sized by volume. Tell the hauler what the structure is made of so they can plan the boxes and avoid an overage surprise.

## Prohibited and Regulated Demolition Materials

Older Southwest Florida structures can contain materials that do not belong in a standard dumpster. Asbestos, found in some older flooring, insulation and siding, is regulated and must be handled and disposed of under specific rules, never put it in a roll-off. Household hazardous waste from the structure, old appliances with refrigerant, and electronics all follow separate Florida disposal routes. If you suspect asbestos in a pre-1980s structure, stop and have it assessed before demolition; the hauler cannot take it in a regular box.

## Placement

Demolition boxes need truck access and, for a tear-down, room to work around the structure. The usual permit rule applies: own property, usually no permit; public right-of-way, usually a permit from the city or county. On a tear-down producing heavy debris, driveway and surface protection matters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What size dumpster do I need for demolition?**
It depends on the structure and its materials. A single-room interior gut may fit a 15- or 20-yard box, a whole-home gut usually needs a 30-yard or swaps, and a lanai or pool-cage tear-down, mostly light aluminum, suits a 20- or 30-yard. Heavy demolition debris like tile, plaster, concrete and block should be separated into a partly-filled 10-yard box. Tell the hauler what the structure is made of so they size by weight and volume together.

**Can I put asbestos in a demolition dumpster?**
No. Asbestos, which can be present in older Southwest Florida flooring, insulation and siding, is a regulated material that must be handled and disposed of under specific rules and cannot go in a standard roll-off. If you suspect asbestos in a pre-1980s structure, have it assessed before demolition begins. A hauler cannot accept it in a regular box, and improper disposal carries serious penalties.

**How do I handle a demolition job with both heavy and light debris?**
Separate the materials into different boxes. Keep dense, heavy debris, tile, plaster, concrete, block, in a partly-filled 10-yard box sized by weight, and put the light, bulky material, drywall, wood, cabinetry, in a larger box sized by volume. A single big box filled with mixed debris can run over its weight limit because of the heavy fraction. Tell the hauler the structure's materials so they plan the boxes.

**What size dumpster for a lanai or pool-cage tear-down?**
A lanai, pool cage or screen enclosure is mostly aluminum framing and screen, bulky but very light, so a 20- or 30-yard box sized by volume usually fits, and weight is rarely the limit. This work is common in Southwest Florida and spikes after storms damage enclosures. If the tear-down also includes a concrete deck or footers, keep that heavy material in a separate partly-filled 10-yard box.
