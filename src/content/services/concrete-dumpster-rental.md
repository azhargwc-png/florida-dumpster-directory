# Concrete & Heavy Debris Dumpster Rental in Southwest Florida, Clean Loads, Weight and Partial Fills

Concrete, block, brick, stone and dirt are the heaviest debris a homeowner or contractor deals with, and they follow their own set of rules. The governing idea is simple: with heavy debris, you rent a small box, fill it only partway, and often keep it "clean" so it can be recycled rather than landfilled. Get that wrong and you pay steep overage charges or get a pickup refused for being overweight. ## Why Heavy Debris Uses a Small Box and a Partial Fill

A single cubic yard of broken concrete can weigh well over a ton. Load that into a large container and you hit the box's included weight allowance, and start paying per-ton overage, when it looks nearly empty, and a badly overweight box may be refused at pickup because the truck cannot safely lift or legally haul it. The standard approach across Southwest Florida is a [10-yard](/10-yard-dumpster-rental/) box filled only to a "fill line" the hauler points out, often half to two-thirds full. It looks like wasted space; it is actually the only way to keep a dense load under the weight limit and moving.

Tell the hauler exactly what the material is, concrete, block, brick, tile, dirt, or a mix, and they will set the fill line and the box size for the weight.

## Clean Loads vs Mixed Loads

A "clean" load is a single dense material with nothing else in it, concrete only, or dirt only. Clean loads matter because clean concrete, block and brick can often be recycled: crushed and reused as fill or aggregate rather than landfilled. A clean, recyclable load is frequently cheaper to dispose of than a mixed one, and in Lee County, where covered construction-and-demolition projects face a 50% recycling-or-reuse requirement, keeping concrete clean helps meet that rule. Mix in wood, trash or other debris and the load loses its clean status, which can raise the disposal cost and rule out recycling. If you are removing a concrete driveway and also clearing other junk, keep them in separate boxes.

## What Counts as Heavy Debris

Concrete, asphalt, brick, block and masonry; stone and pavers; dirt, soil and sod; clay and concrete roof tile; and wet, dense demolition debris. All of these are weight-first materials sized by tonnage, not volume. Lighter mixed debris, furniture, drywall, packaging, general cleanout clutter, is the opposite case and belongs in a larger box sized by volume.

## Placement

A 10-yard heavy-debris box fits most driveways with room to spare, which helps on tight Southwest Florida lots. The usual permit rule applies: own driveway, usually no permit; street, sidewalk or swale, usually a right-of-way permit from the city or county. A box loaded with concrete is extremely heavy, so protecting a paver or sealed driveway with boards under the contact points is especially important here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What size dumpster do I need for concrete?**
A 10-yard box is the standard choice for concrete and other heavy debris, filled only partway to the hauler's fill line. Because a single cubic yard of concrete can weigh over a ton, a larger box would hit its weight limit when it looks nearly empty, triggering overage charges or a refused pickup. Tell the hauler the exact material so they can set the correct box size and fill line for the weight.

**Why can't I fill a concrete dumpster all the way?**
Concrete is so dense that a box reaches its weight limit long before it fills on volume. Filling a concrete dumpster to the top would leave it badly overweight, too heavy for the truck to safely lift and legally haul, so the pickup may be refused, and any weight over the allowance is billed per ton. The hauler sets a fill line, often half to two-thirds full, that keeps the load under the weight limit.

**Can clean concrete be recycled?**
Often, yes. Clean concrete, block and brick, a load with no wood, trash or other debris mixed in, can frequently be crushed and recycled as fill or aggregate rather than landfilled, which is usually cheaper to dispose of. In Lee County, keeping concrete clean also helps meet the 50% construction-and-demolition recycling requirement on covered projects. Ask the hauler whether they recycle clean loads and keep concrete separate from other debris.

**Can I mix concrete with other debris?**
It is better not to. Mixing concrete with wood, trash or general debris removes the load's "clean" status, which can rule out recycling and raise the disposal cost. It also complicates weight sizing. If you are removing concrete and also clearing other material, use separate boxes, a partly-filled 10-yard for the concrete and an appropriately sized box for the rest. Ask the hauler how they prefer the materials separated.
