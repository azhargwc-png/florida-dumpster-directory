# Roofing Dumpster Rental in Southwest Florida, Sizing a Box for Shingle and Tile Tear-Offs

## Shingle vs Tile, Why They Need Different Boxes

Asphalt-shingle debris is moderately heavy but manageable. A typical single-family shingle re-roof fits a [20-yard](/20-yard-dumpster-rental/) box well, filling it on volume at roughly the same time it approaches the weight allowance. That balance is why the 20 is the standard shingle-roof box.

Clay and concrete tile is a different animal. Tile is dense, three to four times heavier per square than shingles, so a tile tear-off hits a dumpster's weight limit when the box looks barely started. Put a tile roof in a 20-yard box and you will pay heavy per-ton overage on a container that appears a third full. The fix is the same as every heavy-debris job in Southwest Florida: a smaller box, often a [10-yard](/10-yard-dumpster-rental/), filled only partway to the hauler's fill line. Many local roofers running tile will tell you a 10-yard box, or multiple small boxes, is the correct call.

## Sizing by Roofing Squares

Roofers measure roofs in "squares", one square equals 100 square feet of roof area. A rough guide, which your hauler will refine:

- A small-to-mid shingle roof (up to about 20 squares) often fits a 15- or 20-yard box.
- A larger shingle roof (20 to 35+ squares) usually calls for a 20-yard box, sometimes with a mid-job swap.
- Any tile roof should be sized by weight, not squares, expect a 10-yard box run to a partial fill, with the hauler setting the fill line based on the tile type.

Tell the company your roof's square count and whether it is shingle, concrete tile or clay tile when you call, so they can size by weight rather than guessing.

## Placement for a Re-Roof

A re-roof means tear-off debris coming off the roof fast, so the box should sit as close to the work as the driveway allows. The standard placement rules apply: your driveway, usually no permit; a street, sidewalk or swale, usually a right-of-way permit from the city or county. Because roofing debris is heavy, driveway protection matters, insist on boards under the contact points on a paver or sealed driveway. If roofers are dropping debris from height, make sure the box placement and the fall zone are coordinated so nothing lands outside the container.

## What to Look for in a Company for a Roofing Dumpster

The weight conversation is everything on a roofing job. Tell the hauler the roof's square count and material, and ask them to size by weight, a company that reflexively quotes a 20-yard box for a tile roof without asking about the material is one to be cautious of. Confirm the included tonnage and the per-ton overage rate, and for tile ask for a clear fill line. On a bigger roof, ask about a mid-job swap. Confirm the debris goes to a facility that accepts roofing waste. Use the directory's review counts alongside the call.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What size dumpster do I need for a roof replacement?**
For a typical single-family shingle re-roof, a 20-yard box is the standard choice and fits most roofs up to about 30 to 35 squares, sometimes with a mid-job swap. For a tile roof, size by weight instead, tile is three to four times heavier than shingles, so a 10-yard box filled partway is usually correct. Tell the hauler your roof's square count and material so they can size the box properly.

**Why does a tile roof need a smaller dumpster than a shingle roof?**
Clay and concrete tile weigh three to four times as much as asphalt shingles for the same roof area, so a tile tear-off reaches a dumpster's weight limit when the box is only a fraction full. A large box filled with tile means heavy per-ton overage charges on a container that looks nearly empty. A 10-yard box filled to the hauler's fill line keeps the load under the weight allowance, which is why tile roofs use smaller boxes.

**What is a roofing square?**
A roofing square is 100 square feet of roof area, the unit roofers use to measure and price a roof. A rough guide: a shingle roof up to about 20 squares often fits a 15- or 20-yard box, and larger shingle roofs usually need a 20-yard box. Tile roofs are sized by weight rather than squares. Giving your hauler the square count and roofing material lets them size the box correctly.

**Can I put old shingles and tile in the same dumpster?**
You can combine them, but it makes sizing harder because the tile's weight dominates. If a roof has both, the hauler should size the box for the total weight, which usually means a smaller container than the volume suggests. Tell the company the mix when you call. Keeping heavy tile in its own partly-filled box is often the cleaner approach on a mixed tear-off.
